# Five New App Concepts — Romantic & Classical Weddings

Five app ideas not currently in the 20-app catalog or the 14 approved concepts. Each is designed for a romantic-classical couple: candlelit, sincere, tradition-anchored, keepsake-first. Each is checked against the Five Rules in `one-pager.md` and the Non-Negotiables and shapes in `app-guidelines.md`.

Each concept is sketched at Content-Brief depth (per `app-guidelines.md § Content Brief For Each New Build`) so the studio can decide whether to promote it to a full approved concept.

---

## 1. The Toasts You Didn't Get To Hear

- **Shape:** Ambient participation → private keepsake.
- **The couple:** Anyone who wants more speeches than the schedule allows without adding thirty minutes to dinner.
- **The moment:** Guests can submit throughout cocktail hour and dinner. Reads happen in curated batches between courses; a final printed book is delivered after the wedding.
- **The guest action:** One two-to-three-sentence written toast, optionally tagged to a moment (arrival, dinner, cake, first dance). Under 60 seconds.
- **The room payoff:** The MC reads two or three approved toasts between courses in a warm, unhurried voice. Ambient — no leaderboard, no reveal drama.
- **The curation model:** Trusted friend or planner approves each toast before the MC sees it. Couple can pre-approve categories of contributors (family, college, work) to speed the queue.
- **The keepsake:** A printed hardcover — "The Toasts You Didn't Get To Hear" — with every submitted toast, attributed or anonymous per guest preference.
- **The fallback:** If submission is thin, the MC quietly stops. The book still ships from whatever came in.
- **The boundary:** No jokes-at-someone's-expense category. Moderation guidelines shipped with the app.

**Why it's romantic-classical:** Reframes speeches from performance to letter-writing. Every quiet guest gets a voice without being called to a mic.

## 2. The Vows They Almost Wrote

- **Shape:** Room game and reveal (in the emotional-outlier sense — like the Love Letter Machine, not competitive).
- **The couple:** Pairs who wrote their own vows and inevitably cut lines they loved but couldn't fit.
- **The moment:** A single ~7-minute segment after dinner, before dancing.
- **The guest action:** Guests see pairs of alternate vow lines ("Line A: from her cutting-room floor / Line B: also from her cutting-room floor") and tap the one they wish had made it into the ceremony. One tap per round, ~5 rounds.
- **The room payoff:** After each round, the big screen reveals which line the room chose and the story behind the cut — often funnier or more tender than the polished version. Ends with the partner who wrote them reading the room's favorite aloud.
- **The curation model:** Couple selects the lines pre-event. No user-generated content live.
- **The keepsake:** A printed booklet with the delivered vows on one page and the room-chosen alternate lines on the facing page.
- **The fallback:** Works with any level of participation — vote counts are illustrative, not decisive.
- **The boundary:** Only lines the couple chose to expose. Nothing scraped from private drafts without explicit approval.

**Why it's romantic-classical:** Elevates the most classical romantic act of the day (vow-writing) into a shared room moment without turning it into a game show.

## 3. The First-Look Voice Letter *(now approved — live at `/apps/first-look-voice-letter`)*

- **Shape:** Private keepsake.
- **The couple:** Introverted or emotionally-driven couples who care more about hearing their people than seeing them dance.
- **The moment:** Available across cocktail hour and dinner, with a soft MC prompt once ("if you have a minute, tell them what you noticed when they walked in").
- **The guest action:** A 15–30 second voice memo answering one of three couple-authored prompts: what you noticed today, what you wish for them, what you remember about them from before. Recorded from the phone, no video.
- **The room payoff:** None live. This is a deliberately quiet app — no wall, no reveal. The prompt-card in the printed program is the only in-room presence.
- **The curation model:** Couple listens post-event; can privately hide any clip. Nothing public at any point.
- **The keepsake:** A stitched-together audio letter (25–40 minutes) delivered as a private podcast link — the couple listens together on their honeymoon or their first Sunday back.
- **The fallback:** If submission is low, the couple gets a shorter letter. The value is per-clip, not aggregate.
- **The boundary:** Voice only, no video. Explicit note that the audio is for the couple and never public.

**Why it's romantic-classical:** The classical "reception videographer interview" reimagined without the tripod, the queue, or the awkward eye-line. Preserves the voices of older relatives especially well.

## 4. The Slow Reveal Bouquet

- **Shape:** Ambient participation → framed keepsake. Replaces the bouquet toss for couples who find it dated or exclusionary.
- **The couple:** Traditional-leaning couples who want the ritual of the bouquet without the toss.
- **The moment:** Contribution across dinner; reveal during the moment where the bouquet toss would have happened.
- **The guest action:** Each guest submits one flower and one line — the flower they'd add, and who they'd add it for ("peony, for my grandmother's garden"). Under 45 seconds.
- **The room payoff:** The big screen shows a bouquet illustration growing across dinner. At the reveal moment, the finished bouquet displays with attribution scrolling gently beside it. The couple carries a physical bouquet in the same composition (florist works from a mid-dinner snapshot) — the live wall matches what the couple holds.
- **The curation model:** Illustration engine auto-composes. Couple's friend can hide any submission before it's added to the bouquet.
- **The keepsake:** Framed illustrated bouquet with every guest's flower and attribution — hung in the couple's home.
- **The fallback:** Pre-seeded partner-authored flowers keep the illustration full even if participation is thin.
- **The boundary:** No political / religious / grief-heavy dedications live without couple pre-approval. Sensitive dedications can be flagged private-only.

**Why it's romantic-classical:** Directly transforms a ceremonial classical act into something every guest — not just single women — participates in. Ends as a heirloom, not a discarded prop.

## 5. The Slow Dance Dedications

- **Shape:** Ambient participation → private keepsake, with one room moment.
- **The couple:** Music-first couples with a curated slow-song set.
- **The moment:** Contribution across dinner; one three-minute segment during the slow-dance portion of dancing.
- **The guest action:** Guests pick a slow song from the couple's pre-approved shortlist and write one sentence — who they're dedicating this song to, at this wedding, right now. ("For my husband, who I met at their engagement party.")
- **The room payoff:** During the slow-dance set, one dedication appears in small type at the bottom of the screen at the start of each song. Room-scanning couples read them across each other's shoulders. No leaderboards, no attribution unless guest chose to sign.
- **The curation model:** Trusted friend approves each dedication before it's queued to a song.
- **The keepsake:** A printed setlist booklet — each song page includes the dedications that guests attached to it. Doubles as the couple's soundtrack keepsake.
- **The fallback:** Any song with zero dedications plays with the screen dark. Nothing feels broken.
- **The boundary:** No live attribution to specific couples in the room without both parties' consent (the "for my ex" problem). Moderation guidance covers this explicitly.

**Why it's romantic-classical:** The dance floor becomes readable. Turns the anonymous slow-dance moment into something the room quietly witnesses together, without interrupting it.

## How these compare to the existing catalog

| Existing app | Adjacent new concept | What's genuinely new |
|---|---|---|
| Love Letter Machine | Toasts You Didn't Get To Hear | Ambient not spectacle; MC-paced not couple-paced; produces a book |
| Advice Oracle | The Vows They Almost Wrote | Couple-authored content, not guest-authored; single hosted reveal |
| Video Guestbook | First-Look Voice Letter | Audio-only, no video queue, delivered as a private podcast |
| — | Slow Reveal Bouquet | No existing app replaces a ceremonial tradition on the dance floor |
| Collaborative Soundtrack (#15, non-live) | Slow Dance Dedications | Dedications not song submissions; anchored to a moment, not the whole night |

## Selection guidance for the studio

If we pilot only one, **The Toasts You Didn't Get To Hear** is the safest bet: it slots into any dinner rhythm, needs no ceremony modifications, produces a hardcover keepsake with clear per-couple pricing logic, and has an obvious sales line ("your MC reads warm two-line notes from guests who never got a mic").

**The Vows They Almost Wrote** is the most tonally distinctive but only works when both partners wrote their own vows — a real disqualifier for a decent share of the ICP.
