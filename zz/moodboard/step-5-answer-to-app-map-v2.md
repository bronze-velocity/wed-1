# Moodboard Step 5 v2: Realistic Questions, Answers, And Outcomes

The first version treated every answer like the seed of an elaborate custom concept. That is not how most couples think about their wedding. Most answers are ordinary: they met at work, their friends tease them about being late, they want everyone to laugh, or they want their parents to feel included.

The job of Step 5 is not to turn one anecdote into an entire app. It is to collect enough familiar material to decide which approved app shape will feel personal when Wepho fills it in. The app remains recognizable; the couple's answer determines the questions, prompts, examples, people, and final reveal.

This version uses simpler questions, common answers, and realistic outcomes. The outcomes are examples of what the matching engine could say, not promises that one answer automatically selects one app.

## Design Principles

- Ask for information couples can answer in ten seconds, not a polished origin story.
- Use ordinary details as customization material. A first date restaurant, a shared TV show, or an argument about being late is enough.
- Describe the guest behavior and room payoff, not just the app feature.
- Offer one clear recommended direction and, when useful, one adjacent option. Do not present three elaborate concepts for every answer.
- Let the rest of the moodboard decide whether the direction should be funny, emotional, social, quiet, active, or keepsake-led.
- Never imply that a sensitive answer needs to be shown publicly. The couple chooses what is appropriate for the room.

## 1. How Did You Meet?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `How did you meet?`

**Helper text:** `A short answer is perfect: work, school, friends, online, or somewhere unexpected.`

### Answer A

> "We met through mutual friends. They kept inviting us to the same dinners until we finally went on a date."

**Likely recommendation:** `Live "How Well Do You Know Us?" Trivia`

**Realistic outcome:** The game includes questions about who introduced them, who noticed the connection first, and what their friends thought before the couple did. The room gets a funny, easy-to-follow story, and the friends who set them up become part of the reveal.

**Adjacent option:** `Who Said It?` using early texts and comments from the friends who saw it coming.

### Answer B

> "We met at work. We were on different teams and started talking because we were both always the last people in the office."

**Likely recommendation:** `Who Said It?`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests guess who wrote short messages from the early days, then see the timeline move from work chat to first date to relationship. The app gives coworkers an easy way into a story they may only know from the wedding invitation.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia` about who made the first move, who knew first, and what each person thought after the first date.

### Answer C

> "We met online. Our first date was coffee, and neither of us expected to see the other again."

**Likely recommendation:** `Who Said It?`

**Realistic outcome:** Use a few approved screenshots, first-date details, and early messages as a short guessing game. Guests learn the real beginning of the relationship without the couple having to give a formal speech about it.

**Adjacent option:** `The Story Chain`, seeded with the simple prompt: `They almost did not go on a second date because...`

## 2. What Do Your Friends Always Tease You About?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `What is one small thing your friends always tease you about?`

**Helper text:** `Think habits, food orders, punctuality, pets, or the thing one of you always does.`

### Answer A

> "I am always late, and he says he has learned to tell me the wrong start time."

**Likely recommendation:** `Custom Wedding Bingo`

**Realistic outcome:** The card includes harmless, observable moments such as someone mentioning the start time, a late arrival to the dance floor, or a friend making the familiar joke. Guests stay alert to the wedding rather than checking their phones continuously.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia` with questions about who is actually late, how late is too late, and which partner is more likely to pretend they are five minutes away.

### Answer B

> "We can never agree on where to eat. Choosing dinner takes longer than making dinner."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`

**Realistic outcome:** The couple supplies a short list of real food and restaurant opinions. Guests vote during cocktail hour, the screen reveals which partner holds each opinion, and nearby strangers have an easy reason to compare their own answers.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia` about the couple's actual food habits and the decision that takes them longest.

### Answer C

> "Our dog is treated like a third person in the relationship."

**Likely recommendation:** `Custom Wedding Bingo`

**Realistic outcome:** The dog becomes a light recurring thread in the card and the display copy, with squares tied to speeches, photos, or guests mentioning who the dog would choose. The joke is understandable to the whole room and does not require an elaborate game premise.

**Adjacent option:** `The Home Your Room Built`, where guests add one feature the couple's future home needs for the dog.

## 3. What Would Make The Wedding Feel Like You?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `What would make you say, "That felt like us" the next morning?`

**Helper text:** `Choose the moment you would most want to remember: a big laugh, a family moment, everyone joining in, or something quiet.`

### Answer A

> "Everyone laughing together, including both families."

**Likely recommendation:** `Live "How Well Do You Know Us?" Trivia`

**Realistic outcome:** A short, host-led game uses accessible questions about the couple, with a few deeper details for the people who know them well. Both families are doing the same thing at the same time, and the final leaderboard gives the laughter a clear ending.

**Adjacent option:** `Who Said It?` if the couple has enough messages, photos, or memorable lines to build a guessing round.

### Answer B

> "A moment with our parents and grandparents that feels genuine, not staged."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`

**Realistic outcome:** Older relatives can record short messages from their seats or with help from a family member. The couple receives an archive of real voices and faces, without requiring anyone to perform on the big screen.

**Adjacent option:** `Anniversary Time Capsule`, with messages assigned to the first or fifth anniversary.

### Answer C

> "Guests who do not know each other actually talking by the end of the night."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests answer quick, harmless questions during cocktail hour and compare answers with people nearby. The app supplies a reason to start talking, then gets out of the way before dinner.

**Adjacent option:** `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story` when the venue has meaningful spaces and the couple wants guests moving around.

## 4. What Do You Two Disagree About?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `What is one harmless thing you never agree on?`

**Helper text:** `Food, travel, routines, music, films, or who is right about the thermostat all work.`

### Answer A

> "We disagree about whether to plan every part of a holiday or just arrive and see what happens."

**Likely recommendation:** `Where To Next`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests add destinations and short reasons, grouped into the couple's existing travel styles. The room sees the map grow, and the couple leaves with suggestions they could actually use together.

**Adjacent option:** `The Prediction Vault`, asking where the couple will travel or live in five years.

### Answer B

> "He thinks mornings should be quiet. I think breakfast is the best time to talk about everything."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`

**Realistic outcome:** The couple pre-loads a set of real daily-life opinions, not just this one. Guests vote quickly, the screen shows whether the room sides with either partner, and the results give tables an easy conversation.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia` about which partner has which habit and who is most likely to change their mind.

### Answer C

> "We are always debating whether to save money or book the nicer version of a trip."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Prediction Vault`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests make light, future-facing predictions about the couple's next big trip, future home, or spending habits. The predictions are sealed for a later anniversary, so the disagreement becomes an ongoing joke rather than a public argument.

**Adjacent option:** `Where To Next`, framed around the places guests think are worth splurging on.

## 5. What Might Surprise Your Guests About You?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `What is something your guests might not know about you two?`

**Helper text:** `Keep it light or skip it. A different personality, a long-distance chapter, or an unexpected shared interest are all useful.`

### Answer A

> "People think we are very different, but we are both much more competitive than we look."

**Likely recommendation:** `Live "How Well Do You Know Us?" Trivia`

**Realistic outcome:** The game reveals the couple's competitive side through a few well-written questions, a visible score, and a small winner's moment. It lets the room discover the surprise through play instead of being told in a speech.

**Adjacent option:** `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker` for a lighter, cocktail-hour version.

### Answer B

> "We have been together for ten years, but most of the room only knows the last few years."

**Likely recommendation:** `Who Said It?`

**Realistic outcome:** A chronological set of messages, photos, or short quotes lets guests see the relationship change over time. The result is a concise story of the ten years, not a slideshow of every photograph the couple owns.

**Adjacent option:** `Anniversary Time Capsule` if the couple wants the experience to look forward instead of back.

### Answer C

> "We are actually quite private. The people here know us well, but we do not want a big public performance."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests record when it suits them, with the couple choosing who can view the clips. The app creates a meaningful archive without making the couple or their guests the center of a live show.

**Adjacent option:** `The Guest Advice Oracle`, with a private archive and a carefully selected public display.

## 6. What Is Something You Always Do Together?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `What is one routine or tradition you share?`

**Helper text:** `A weekly meal, annual trip, favorite show, Sunday routine, or small way you look after each other.`

### Answer A

> "We make breakfast together every Sunday, even when we are traveling."

**Likely recommendation:** `Custom Wedding Bingo`

**Realistic outcome:** The routine becomes a recognizable thread through the wedding, with guests spotting references to breakfast, travel, and the couple's usual Sunday roles. It adds personality without asking guests to understand an inside joke first.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia` with questions about who cooks, who chooses the music, and which Sunday breakfast went wrong.

### Answer B

> "We take a walk after dinner every night and use it to catch up."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Guest Advice Oracle`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests answer a small set of prompts about how they stay connected in ordinary life. The couple gets an advice archive that reflects the kind of everyday relationship they value.

**Adjacent option:** `Anniversary Time Capsule`, asking guests what they hope the couple still makes time for years from now.

### Answer C

> "We plan one trip every year, even if it is only a weekend nearby."

**Likely recommendation:** `Where To Next`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests add destinations that fit the couple's real travel style, from nearby weekends to ambitious future trips. The finished map becomes a useful list of places and the reasons each guest recommended them.

**Adjacent option:** `The Prediction Vault`, with guests predicting the destination of the couple's next anniversary trip.

## 7. What Song, Place, Or Thing Reminds You Of Each Other?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `What song, place, or everyday thing feels most like you two?`

**Helper text:** `It does not have to be romantic. A restaurant, a neighborhood, a pet, a film, or an object from your first home all work.`

### Answer A

> "The restaurant where we had our first date. We still go there for big decisions."

**Likely recommendation:** `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`

**Realistic outcome:** Meaningful places become the structure for a short cocktail-hour experience. If the real restaurant is not at the venue, the story can be echoed through stops that each represent a decision or chapter.

**Adjacent option:** `Where To Next`, putting the first-date restaurant beside future places the couple wants to visit.

### Answer B

> "A song from the summer we started dating. Everyone in the wedding party knows it."

**Likely recommendation:** `Custom Wedding Bingo`

**Realistic outcome:** The song can become an observable wedding-day moment, surrounded by squares about the summer, the wedding party, and what happens when the song plays. The app supports the real reception rather than asking guests to manage a music queue.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia`, using the song as one clue in a round about that summer.

### Answer C

> "Our old apartment. It was tiny, but it was the first place that felt like ours."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Home Your Room Built`

**Realistic outcome:** Guests build a visual version of the couple's next home, adding practical features, familiar objects, and one or two running preferences. The live illustration becomes a framed keepsake that connects the first home to the life ahead.

**Adjacent option:** `The Guest Advice Oracle`, with prompts about making a home together.

## 8. Who Knows You Best?

### Suggested question

**Primary:** `Who in the room knows your relationship best?`

**Helper text:** `Name a person or group, and tell us what they have seen: the early days, the difficult years, or the everyday version of you.`

### Answer A

> "Our siblings. They have seen every stage and will definitely disagree about which stories are accurate."

**Likely recommendation:** `Who Said It?`

**Realistic outcome:** Siblings become named sources in a short, moderated guessing game. Guests guess who said each line, then see the different perspectives converge on the same relationship.

**Adjacent option:** `Live Trivia`, with a family-versus-friends leaderboard.

### Answer B

> "Our oldest friends. They know how we were before we became a couple and remember all the early mistakes."

**Likely recommendation:** `Live "How Well Do You Know Us?" Trivia`

**Realistic outcome:** The questions move from early friendship to relationship milestones, with a few accessible questions for newer guests. The oldest friends get bragging rights without turning the night into an insider-only event.

**Adjacent option:** `The Story Chain`, opening with a true early memory and letting the room add its own version.

### Answer C

> "Our parents. They know the family stories, but they do not know much about our everyday life together."

**Likely recommendation:** `The Guest Advice Oracle`

**Realistic outcome:** Different generations answer prompts about what they have learned, what they hope the couple remembers, and what ordinary habit makes a relationship last. The couple gets a thoughtful keepsake while guests learn something about one another.

**Adjacent option:** `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`, especially when hearing parents' and grandparents' voices is the main value.

## What The Matching Engine Should Do With These Answers

Step 5 should extract a few practical signals rather than overfit to the wording:

- **Story source:** couple, friends, family, coworkers, or mixed guests.
- **Story depth:** first meeting, early relationship, everyday life, or future plans.
- **Content available:** messages, photos, opinions, places, voices, routines, or none yet.
- **Preferred social shape:** a whole-room game, ambient participation, movement, a shared creation, or a private keepsake.
- **Emotional temperature:** funny, warm, sentimental, competitive, or low-key.
- **Publicness:** happy to show it live, curate carefully, keep it private, or unlock it later.

The output should sound like a practical design recommendation:

> `You have a ten-year story and siblings who remember every stage. Who Said It? could turn a few approved texts, photos, and quotes into a ten-minute dinner game, with the room guessing who said each thing before the real context appears.`

It should not sound like a demand to invent an entirely new product:

> `Because your siblings disagree about the early years, we will build a never-before-seen sibling chronology courtroom.`

The first statement uses an approved app, names the material the couple can provide, explains the guest action, and describes the room outcome. That is the level of specificity Step 5 needs.
