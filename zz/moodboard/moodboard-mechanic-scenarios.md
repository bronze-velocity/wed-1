# Moodboard mechanic — 15 scenarios

Fifteen concrete walkthroughs of a couple (or planner) using the reimagined moodboard. Each scenario shows what they enter, what the tool throws back in real time, and the one moment they screenshot.

Guiding constraints, pulled from `zz/one-pager.md`:

- **Phone in, room out.** Every suggestion has to land in the room, not on a screen alone.
- **Built for one couple, one night.** No off-the-shelf. The tool's job is to make couples *feel* the specificity, not just tag it.
- **The couple curates.** Ideas the tool generates are always drafts the couple approves, never edicts.
- **Sweet spot: 60–200 guests.** Anything outside the tool should say so honestly.
- **Specificity creates believability.** Reactive commentary must reference the answer they just gave, in their words.
- **Wow moment early.** The rail should surprise them by step 2, not by the end.

Two shared mechanics referenced below:

- **Ideas rail.** Sticky right column that fills with app-idea cards as they answer. Each card has three buttons: pin, dismiss, make it weirder.
- **Fit panel.** Two lists that live-populate: *Would work at your wedding* and *Probably not for you*.

---

## 1. The Grandma-heavy wedding

**Couple:** Sam and Priya, 60 guests, average age tilts old.
**They enter:** *"Half the room is our parents' friends. My nan is 93 and coming."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail card: **Grandma's Playlist.** Elders record 20-second voice notes before the day. During dinner, one plays per course, and the room guesses whose voice it is.
- Fit panel adds "voice-first inputs" to the green column, "anything requiring three-tap games" to the red.
- Commentary line: *"With Nan at 93, we'd design around listening, not tapping. That opens up a whole family of ideas most couples never see."*

**Wow moment:** the specific card that mentions Nan by name.

---

## 2. The chaos-friend group

**Couple:** Two ex-improv-troupe friends marrying. Guest list is 80% funny.
**They enter:** *"If you gave our friends a mic, we'd never get it back."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Rolling Roast.** Every 20 minutes during dinner a new roast prompt appears on the big screen. Guests text in. The couple's chosen best hits the wall live.
- "Make it weirder" turns it into: **The Roast Bracket.** 16 submissions get voted down until a champion roast is crowned during speeches.
- Fit panel red-flags "quiet contemplative apps."

**Wow moment:** the tool responds with a whole *tournament format* they hadn't even asked for.

---

## 3. The destination wedding of strangers

**Couple:** Marrying in Tuscany. Guests from four countries, most have never met each other.
**They enter:** *"Half our people are showing up to a group of strangers."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Six-Degrees Board.** Guests text in one true fact about the couple. The big screen quietly connects the facts through shared guests, drawing a live web of "who knows whom via whom."
- Wildcard prompt: *"If two guests who've never met had to bond in 90 seconds, what topic would work?"*
- Fit panel greens: "small-group triggers." Reds: "apps that assume prior friendships."

**Wow moment:** a card that solves a social problem, not just a decoration problem.

---

## 4. The interfaith blend

**Couple:** Muslim bride, Jewish groom. Two family halves, two languages, one dance floor.
**They enter:** *"Both families are watching each other more than watching us."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **Two Toasts, One Screen.** During speeches, each side submits one-line blessings in their own language. The screen shows both, side by side, translated by the couple's chosen witnesses.
- Card: **Song of the House.** Each family votes on the one song from their side the other family must learn to dance to. Screen teaches the steps.
- Commentary: *"You're managing two audiences. We'd build a paired-input pattern, so every moment shows both sides at once."*

**Wow moment:** a paired-language app format they'd never have imagined asking for.

---

## 5. The blended family

**Couple:** Second marriage. Three kids between them, ages 6, 9, and 14.
**They enter:** *"The kids are in the wedding. They matter more than any guest."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Family We're Making.** Big screen prompt: guests write one hope for the new family, addressed to the kids. Read aloud by the eldest during dessert.
- Fit panel reds: "late-night dance-floor games (the 6-year-old will be asleep)," "adult-humor voting apps."
- Wildcard: *"What's a small ritual we could invent tonight that the kids will remember when they're 30?"*

**Wow moment:** the tool refusing the roast-style ideas because of the 6-year-old is what earns their trust.

## 6. The 20-person micro-wedding

**Couple:** Elopement-adjacent. 20 guests in a converted barn.
**They enter:** *"We wanted 200. We're doing 20. Each person matters."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Fit panel immediately grays out "big-screen live wall" and shows a note: *"At 20 people, the room doesn't need a projector. Passing a tablet works better."*
- Rail: **The Twenty Letters.** Each guest writes a letter to the couple *during* the reception, on a shared surface. Printed and bound before they leave.
- Card: **One Question Each.** Every guest is randomly assigned one question to answer aloud at the table. All answers appear in the keepsake book.

**Wow moment:** the tool cheerfully talking them *out* of the flashy version.

---

## 7. The 400-person Indian wedding

**Couple:** 400 confirmed. Sangeet, ceremony, reception across two days.
**They enter their guest count.**
**Tool reacts (honestly):**
- Fit panel puts a soft-orange note at the top: *"400 is above our sweet spot. Most of our apps assume everyone can see one screen. Two possible paths from here."*
- Rail: **The Table-Cluster Pattern.** Splits the crowd into 25 clusters of 16. Each cluster gets its own mini-app-instance on a table iPad. Clusters cross-pollinate through a shared big screen only during the four biggest beats.
- Rail: **The Sangeet-only version.** One app just for the sangeet, where 400 is an asset, not a problem.

**Wow moment:** the honesty. They wouldn't have expected a sales page to tell them they're too big.

## 8. The Hinge couple with a joke about it

**Couple:** Met on Hinge. Both slightly embarrassed. Very online friend group.
**They enter:** *"Please make the origin story funny, not saccharine."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Rejected Prompts Exhibit.** Every Hinge prompt they *didn't* pick, screenshotted, framed like a museum wall. Guests scan a QR under each and vote which one would have led to a worse marriage.
- Card: **Swipe History Live.** During cocktails, 30 fake anonymized profiles appear on the big screen. Guests swipe. At the end, the couple reveals which ones were real friends of theirs.
- Commentary: *"You told us funny not saccharine, so we won't suggest the letter-machine ideas. Say the word and we'll bring them back."*

**Wow moment:** the tool explicitly withholding a whole idea family based on one line they typed.

---

## 9. The rural no-wifi wedding

**Couple:** Marrying in a hay barn in Devon. Venue Wi-Fi is a rumor.
**They enter:** *"Signal is bad. Really bad."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Fit panel goes red on: "live big-screen submission apps," "anything requiring guest phones to load in the moment."
- Rail: **The Paper Ballot with a Digital Reveal.** Guests write on paper cards during dinner. One trusted friend photographs them at the end. The compiled reveal happens as a video the couple watches on their honeymoon.
- Rail: **Pre-loaded Trivia.** All 15 questions cached on each guest's phone the moment they scan the QR at the venue entrance. Works fully offline for the rest of the night.
- Commentary: *"No signal means the room is the whole computer. We'd lean into that."*

**Wow moment:** the tool inventing a *hybrid paper-and-digital* app because of the constraint they mentioned.

---

## 10. The dance-floor-forever wedding

**Couple:** DJ-fronted. Reception ends at 3am.
**They enter:** *"If people leave the dance floor, we've failed."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Vote-to-Play Wall.** Every 20 minutes the DJ shows three next-song candidates on the big screen. Whoever is on the dance floor votes with their phone. Loudest votes win.
- Rail: **Last Song Standing.** Guests predict the actual final song of the night. Reveal at 2:55am. Prize is bragging rights.
- Wildcard: *"Which one song would empty every table in your family?"*

**Wow moment:** the DJ becoming a live interactive character in the app, not an obstacle to it.

---

## 11. The quiet dinner-party wedding

**Couple:** 40 guests, sit-down 5-course dinner, no dance floor.
**They enter:** *"We want it to feel like the best dinner party we've ever thrown."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Fit panel greens: "table-scale prompts." Reds: "big-screen submission walls."
- Rail: **The Course Card.** Each of the five courses arrives with a small printed card on the table. QR on the back opens a single prompt for that course only. Answers go into a shared table diary.
- Rail: **The Toast-per-Table Relay.** One person from each table gives a two-sentence toast between courses. Order chosen by the app to build a narrative the couple pre-authored.
- Commentary: *"Dinner-party energy needs quiet mechanics. Every card here is designed to not interrupt a conversation."*

**Wow moment:** *"designed to not interrupt a conversation."* They didn't know that was a design brief they could ask for.

---

## 12. The sports-fan couple

**Couple:** Season-ticket holders. Half the guest list is their fantasy league.
**They enter:** *"Our friend group would bet on which speech goes longest."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Wedding Bracket.** 16 competitive events across the night: longest speech, most tears, first drunk uncle. Guests submit predictions. Live leaderboard on the big screen.
- Rail: **The Fantasy Draft.** Pre-wedding, each guest drafts a roster of five other guests they think will do specific things (make a fool of themselves, cry, leave first). Points accrue live.
- "Make it weirder" turns the bracket into a full ESPN-style broadcast with a friend hired as the commentator.

**Wow moment:** the tool inviting them to hire a *friend as commentator*. That kind of idea shift is where a $2k app justifies itself.

---

## 13. The memorial-thread wedding

**Couple:** Bride's father died two years ago. She wants him present but doesn't want the room heavy.
**She enters:** *"My dad. Present but not dominant."*
**Tool reacts (carefully):**
- Rail: **The Empty Chair Message.** One chair at the head table. QR on the chair back. Any guest who knew him can leave a 20-second voice memo across the night. Compiled into a keepsake the couple listens to alone, the next morning.
- Rail: **The One Song.** His favorite song plays exactly once, unannounced, when the couple chooses. No screen moment, no acknowledgement in the app.
- Commentary: *"Present but not dominant, so nothing here goes on the big screen without your call. You approve every second of visibility."*

**Wow moment:** the couple realizing this tool won't force emotion. That's rare and it lands hard.

## 14. The comedy-writer couple

**Couple:** Both write for a late-night show. Terrifyingly funny friend group.
**They enter:** *"The pressure to be funny at our wedding is genuinely stressful."*
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail: **The Writers' Room.** Guests submit joke setups all night. The couple's chosen best five are performed at midnight by a rotating cast of friends, cold-read from the big screen.
- Rail: **The Parallel Universe Game.** 10 alternate-history "what if we hadn't met" scenarios, guests vote on the worst one. Winner performed as a two-minute skit by whichever friend volunteers.
- Commentary: *"You told us the pressure is stressful. Every suggestion here takes the joke off you and puts it on the room."*

**Wow moment:** the tool naming their emotional problem back to them and then solving it.

---

## 15. The planner-led design-forward wedding

**User:** A wedding planner running the wizard on behalf of a client.
**They enter:** planner role via `?role=planner`, then a compressed set of client answers.
**Tool reacts:**
- Rail cards render with an extra field: **spec notes**, aimed at the planner, not the couple.
- Fit panel adds a third column: **Coordinates with existing plan** (lighting cues, MC script beats, timeline touchpoints).
- Final output isn't a couple's brief. It's a two-page **planner spec**: three ranked concepts, tech dependencies, staffing needs, moderation window, and a suggested MC line for each.

**Wow moment:** the tool switching artifact type based on who's driving. A planner opens the output and sees their own document format.

## What ties all 15 together

- Every scenario **references specific words the user typed** in the commentary. That is the only thing that makes it feel alive rather than templated.
- Every scenario has at least one **honest red flag**. Trust is built on the tool refusing bad ideas, not just producing good ones.
- Every scenario produces at least one **card the couple would not have thought of**. That's the wow. The catalog is the floor, not the ceiling.
- Every scenario ends with an **artifact worth sharing**: a rail, a brief, or a planner spec. The wizard's job is to hand the user something they want to send to someone.

## What this implies for the build

1. The reactive-commentary pattern library needs enough hand-authored branches to hit the 15 archetypes above. Start there. Do not go to a model until this shape holds.
2. Red-flag logic is a first-class feature, not a nice-to-have. It's what makes the greens credible.
3. The rail card component needs three modes from day one: pin, dismiss, make it weirder. Skipping "make it weirder" removes the playfulness that makes any of this feel creative rather than clinical.
4. The planner-mode artifact (scenario 15) is different enough to warrant its own results component. Design it now, even if you ship it later.
