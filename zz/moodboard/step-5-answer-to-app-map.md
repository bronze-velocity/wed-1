# Moodboard Step 5: Answer-to-App Map

Step 5 should help couples understand why the questions matter. The answer is not meant to select one app mechanically. It gives Wepho raw material for a specific premise, then the other moodboard steps decide the scale, timing, energy, and guest action.

The examples below use the eight prompts currently rendered by `components/moodboard/steps/StepStory.js`. They are written in the voice of common couple or planner answers, followed by app directions that could plausibly come from that answer. All named catalog apps are from the 13 currently non-skipped apps.

## How To Read The Recommendations

- **Primary direction** is the strongest app match for the answer by itself.
- **Alternate direction** is a second app that uses the same material differently.
- **Custom angle** is the detail that makes the recommendation belong to this couple instead of becoming a generic wedding activity.
- A recommendation should still be checked against guest profile, moment, and desired feeling. For example, a great story can fuel `Who Said It?`, but a quiet multigenerational wedding may need the same story in a `Video Guestbook` instead.

## 1. How Did You Meet?

### Answer A: Dating app, DM, or online beginning

> "We matched on Hinge. I ignored his first message for three days, then replied because he made a joke about a dog in one of my photos."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Build a chronological round from the profile prompt, first messages, the ignored message, and the first date recap. Guests guess who wrote each line, then the couple reveals the context.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Seed the room with "Their love story nearly ended before it started when..." and let guests write the increasingly ridiculous version of the three-day delay.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Ask precise questions such as which profile detail got the first reply, who suggested the first date, and what each person thought after the first meeting.
- **Custom angle:** Make the awkward digital beginning the charm. Do not sanitize it into "you met online"; the missed message and the dog photo are the material.

### Answer B: Introduced by friends, family, or coworkers

> "Our best friends kept putting us in the same room until we finally noticed. The wedding party has been waiting for this longer than we have."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Include the matchmaker's messages, the friends' predictions, and the first time each partner admitted there was something there. Add a matchmaker leaderboard for the guests who called it first.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Split the room into the people who engineered the introduction and everyone else. Questions can reveal who knew first, who gave bad advice, and which friend deserves credit.
- **Alternate direction: Ask Us Anything** Let guests submit the questions they have always wanted answered about the setup, then have the couple take a short, moderated segment at dessert.
- **Custom angle:** The people who introduced them are characters, not background context. Name the instigators and let the app give them a visible role.

### Answer C: Travel, work, school, or a near-miss

> "We met because my flight was delayed and I took the only empty seat at a conference dinner. If the original flight had left on time, we would never have spoken."

- **Primary direction: Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story** Turn the reception into a sequence of near-miss chapters. Each venue location carries one fork in the story, ending with the choice that finally put them at the same table.
- **Alternate direction: Where To Next** Put the places from the origin story on one map layer, then let guests add the next places the couple should go together.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Start with the missed flight, empty seat, or wrong classroom and let guests write alternate explanations before the couple reveals what really happened.
- **Custom angle:** Build around the hinge moment, not just the location. The app should make guests feel how easily this couple could have missed each other.

## 2. What's A Joke Or Reference That Only Your People Would Get?

### Answer A: A pet, food, or domestic habit

> "Everyone knows that Sam cannot be trusted to cook rice, and that the cat gets invited into every serious conversation."

- **Primary direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Make squares observable during the reception: someone mentions the rice, the cat appears in a speech, Sam gives unsolicited cooking advice, or a guest says the couple should get a rice cooker.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Use the domestic mythology as questions with plausible wrong answers: who ruined the first dinner, what does the cat actually respond to, and which kitchen rule has never survived a week?
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Seed a shared story with "The cat knew the rice was doomed when..." and let each table add one sentence.
- **Custom angle:** The joke needs a way in for guests who do not know it. Pair the insider reference with a short reveal so the room learns why it is funny.

### Answer B: A legendary friend-group incident

> "Our friends still say 'Lisbon scooter' whenever we make a bad decision. Nobody needs more explanation than that."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Use the texts, photos, and voice notes from the incident as a guessing round. The reveal can show the exact moment the phrase entered the group's vocabulary.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Let guests reconstruct the scooter story one sentence at a time, with the couple's real ending held back until the last chapter.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Ask who was there, what went wrong first, and which detail has been exaggerated every time the story is told.
- **Custom angle:** Treat the phrase as a shared password. The app can reveal the story to newer friends without making the older friends sit through a full explanation.

### Answer C: A niche fandom, quote, or recurring phrase

> "We say 'classic Tuesday' whenever something is objectively chaotic, because of a TV show only our college friends watched with us."

- **Primary direction: Live Trivia** Use the phrase as the wrapper for questions about the couple's real life, not trivia about the show. Every answer gets a short "classic Tuesday" reaction from the couple.
- **Alternate direction: The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker** Turn the reference into adjacent opinions the whole room can answer, then reveal which partner holds each one.
- **Alternate direction: Who Said It?** Mix lines from the show with actual couple messages and let guests guess which is real, who said it, and when.
- **Custom angle:** Use the fandom as a visual language, but keep the content about the couple. Guests should be able to participate even if they have never seen the show.

## 3. What Would Make You Say "That Was So Us" The Next Morning?

### Answer A: A very specific, room-wide laugh

> "If my brother finally tells the story about the broken canoe and my grandmother corrects every detail from her table, that would be completely us."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Build a short reveal around conflicting versions of the canoe story. Guests guess whether each detail came from the bride, groom, brother, or grandmother.
- **Alternate direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Seed the card with the harmless things that tend to happen at this wedding, including the brother starting the story and grandmother correcting it.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Let tables add the version of the story they believe, then display the couple's actual version as the final line.
- **Custom angle:** The desired moment already contains the cast and the payoff. Recommend an app that stages those people, rather than a generic "funny guest interaction."

### Answer B: A quiet moment with one important person

> "I want my grandfather to leave us a message when he thinks nobody is watching. That is the part I know I will remember."

- **Primary direction: The Two-Minute Video Guestbook** Give the grandfather an assisted, low-pressure recording prompt from his seat, while preserving the clip separately from the highlight film.
- **Alternate direction: Anniversary Time Capsule** Let him choose the year when the message opens. The future date makes the recording feel like a gift, not a required guestbook task.
- **Alternate direction: The Guest Advice Oracle** Ask older relatives a prompt such as "What should they remember when life gets ordinary?" and preserve the signed and anonymous answers in separate editions.
- **Custom angle:** Keep the emotional moment private unless the couple explicitly wants a public reveal. The strongest recommendation may be the one that does not put the grandfather on the big screen.

### Answer C: Everyone doing one small, surprising thing together

> "We want to look up and realize every table has added something to the same picture, and then see the finished thing before the dancing starts."

- **Primary direction: The Home Your Room Built** Guests choose a concrete feature, object, pet, or rule for the couple's imagined home. The illustration assembles live on the screen and becomes a framed poster.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Give every guest one sentence in a shared, bounded story so the room creates a single artifact together instead of a collection of unrelated messages.
- **Alternate direction: Where To Next** Let every table place one future destination or ritual on a map, with clusters and reasons appearing as the room contributes.
- **Custom angle:** The important phrase is "the same picture." Recommend a shared visual payoff, not eight separate guest submissions shown in a feed.

## 4. What's A Running Debate You Two Never Resolve?

### Answer A: A harmless food or household argument

> "Is cereal a soup? Also, who is responsible for replacing the toilet roll? We have taken both questions very seriously for six years."

- **Primary direction: The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker** Pre-load the real stances, let the room vote, and reveal which partner is responsible for each position. The room can see whether it sides with the couple or rejects both of them.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Make the debate a fast couple-knowledge round: who argues which side, what is the oldest evidence, and who secretly changed their mind?
- **Alternate direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Put the debate into the night as an observable running joke, such as a guest reopening it during dinner or someone producing evidence.
- **Custom angle:** Keep stakes playful and specific. This is a room icebreaker, not a referendum on the couple's values.

### Answer B: Travel plans, where to live, or the next big decision

> "She wants to move to the coast. I want to live near a train station. Every holiday becomes a property search."

- **Primary direction: Where To Next** Let guests pin places that fit the couple's actual criteria, with a reason attached to every pin. The final map becomes a useful artifact rather than a generic world map.
- **Alternate direction: The Prediction Vault** Seal predictions for where the couple will live in one, five, and ten years, then reopen and score them on anniversaries.
- **Alternate direction: Ask Us Anything** Invite the room to ask about the debate, but let the couple choose a small, moderated set and pass on questions that feel too personal.
- **Custom angle:** Turn the disagreement into a future decision the couple can revisit. Do not reduce it to "bride versus groom" if the real tension is about a life they are designing together.

### Answer C: A meaningful future question

> "We disagree about how much tradition to keep. I want the old family rituals; he wants us to invent our own."

- **Primary direction: The Guest Advice Oracle** Ask guests which traditions are worth carrying forward and what new ritual the couple should start. Separate practical advice from sentimental answers.
- **Alternate direction: The Prediction Vault** Ask guests to predict which traditions will still exist in ten years and which new ritual will become the one everyone remembers.
- **Alternate direction: Ask Us Anything** Let guests submit questions about how the couple makes decisions, with a clear moderation boundary and a pass option for the couple.
- **Custom angle:** Mark this as a potentially sensitive branch. The app should give the couple control over which parts become public and should never manufacture a fight for entertainment.

## 5. What's Something Your Guests Would Be Shocked To Learn About You Two?

### Answer A: The origin is much less romantic than people assume

> "We met on a dating app, but we told both families we met through mutual friends for almost a year."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Use the old profile prompts, first messages, and the moment the cover story collapsed. Guests vote on who wrote or said each line before the full reveal.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Ask guests to write the fake version first, then let the couple reveal the real beginning as the final sentence.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Make the surprise a series of precise reveals: who swiped first, who lied about having read the profile, and who knew the family would ask questions.
- **Custom angle:** Build toward the confession, not a gotcha. The surprise should make the couple more knowable, not expose private material they did not choose to share.

### Answer B: The polished couple has a ridiculous private life

> "Everyone thinks we are calm and organized. At home we communicate almost entirely through badly performed animal noises."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Collect real messages and voice-note transcripts that show the private language, then let guests decide which partner started each one.
- **Alternate direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Put low-stakes versions of the private habits into the day, such as a specific noise, phrase, or gesture appearing in a speech or photo.
- **Alternate direction: Ask Us Anything** Let the room ask the questions that expose the gap between the public couple and the home couple, with the couple choosing what to answer.
- **Custom angle:** The contrast is the hook: composed in public, absurd in private. Give guests a safe, curated window into that contrast instead of asking them to submit their own embarrassing material.

### Answer C: A meaningful family, cultural, or life detail

> "We speak three languages between us, and our families have completely different ideas about what a proper celebration looks like."

- **Primary direction: Where To Next** Map the places, languages, and family homes that shaped the couple. Guests can add one place they want the couple to return to or discover together.
- **Alternate direction: The Two-Minute Video Guestbook** Invite each family to leave a short message in the language that feels most natural, with captions or a translation supplied by a trusted person.
- **Alternate direction: The Guest Advice Oracle** Use prompts about the traditions guests hope the couple keeps, combines, or invents, with explicit visibility controls for private answers.
- **Custom angle:** Do not turn cultural difference into a novelty reveal. The app should show two worlds being joined and let the couple decide what gets translated, displayed, or kept private.

## 6. What's A Ritual Or Tradition That Only The Two Of You Share?

### Answer A: A weekly food, film, or morning ritual

> "Every Sunday we make pancakes, listen to the same album, and rank the week as if we are running a tiny restaurant."

- **Primary direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Turn the ritual into a set of affectionate observations guests can spot during the day, such as a specific album, pancake reference, or fake restaurant review.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Ask who started the ritual, who breaks the rules, and which Sunday had the worst review. The couple's real answers become the reveal.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Start with the ritual and let the room invent the disastrous Sunday when the tradition nearly ended.
- **Custom angle:** Preserve the rhythm and vocabulary of the ritual. A generic "tell us about your traditions" prompt is weaker than the couple's actual ranking system.

### Answer B: A place-based or travel ritual

> "Whenever we visit a new city, we find the worst-looking restaurant, order the house specialty, and leave a review for each other."

- **Primary direction: Where To Next** Guests add the next cities and restaurants the couple should test, while the couple's existing ritual forms the map's legend.
- **Alternate direction: Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story** Hide chapters around the venue as if it were one of the couple's travel days, with each stop revealing the next rule in the ritual.
- **Alternate direction: The Prediction Vault** Ask guests to predict the place, meal, or travel mishap that will become the couple's next five-year story.
- **Custom angle:** The ritual is a format, not just a fact. Let guests participate by making the next recommendation or chapter in the same style.

### Answer C: A private ritual about care, family, or the future

> "Before bed we each say one good thing that happened, even on a terrible day. We want to keep doing it when life gets busy."

- **Primary direction: Anniversary Time Capsule** Ask guests to leave one good thing they hope the couple notices in their first, fifth, or tenth year, then seal the messages by anniversary.
- **Alternate direction: The Two-Minute Video Guestbook** Use the ritual as the prompt: "What is one good thing you hope they still notice about each other?" Keep recordings private and low pressure.
- **Alternate direction: The Guest Advice Oracle** Ask long-married guests how they protect a small ritual when the rest of life gets loud, producing a useful archive rather than generic advice.
- **Custom angle:** Match the quietness of the ritual. A public leaderboard would work against the answer; the value is repetition, care, and a keepsake the couple can return to.

## 7. What's A Song, Place, Or Object That Instantly Means "Us"?

### Answer A: A song with a specific story

> "'Dancing Queen' was playing when we got locked out of our flat, and now it is the only song that can fix a bad day."

- **Primary direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Make the song a possible event in the room, then surround it with squares that reference the actual lockout story and the friends who were there.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Ask guests to identify which song, night, or bad decision belongs to which chapter of the relationship. The song becomes an audio cue for the reveal.
- **Alternate direction: The Two-Minute Video Guestbook** Prompt guests to leave the couple a memory connected to the song, with a short excerpt or title used as the private chapter heading.
- **Custom angle:** The song needs its incident. Recommend the story around it, not a generic music-voting app, especially when the approved catalog does not include a dedicated soundtrack format.

### Answer B: A place that holds a chapter

> "The corner booth at a restaurant in Leeds. We sat there on our first date, and we still go there whenever we need to make a serious decision."

- **Primary direction: Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story** Recreate the place-based feeling through venue stops, with each location carrying a decision or chapter from the couple's timeline.
- **Alternate direction: Where To Next** Put the booth and other meaningful places on a private map, then let guests add places where the couple should make future decisions.
- **Alternate direction: The Relationship Origin Story Exhibit** If this app is brought into the build, make the place the anchor of a chronological exhibit with photos, voice notes, and the story of the first decision made there. This is a custom extension rather than one of the 13 current apps.
- **Custom angle:** Ask what happened there and why they return, not only for the address. The emotional function of the place determines whether the right output is movement, a map, or a quiet exhibit.

### Answer C: An object, pet, or ordinary thing with disproportionate meaning

> "A chipped blue mug. It was the only thing we owned together for the first year, and whoever finds it in a box gets to choose the takeaway."

- **Primary direction: Live Trivia** Build a round around the object's history, including who bought it, when it nearly got thrown away, and the current takeaway rule.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Give the mug a voice and let guests write the account of how it survived moves, breakups-that-were-not-breakups, and three kitchen renovations.
- **Alternate direction: The Home Your Room Built** Let guests add the mug, the takeaway rule, and other objects or rules to the home illustration, turning one small artifact into a picture of the couple's shared life.
- **Custom angle:** Ordinary objects are powerful because they carry time. Use the object's chronology and rule, not just its photograph.

## 8. Who In The Room Has The Best Story About You Two, And What Is It?

### Answer A: A sibling or parent who was there from the beginning

> "My sister saw us after our first date and knew before either of us did. She tells the story better, and she has the screenshots to prove it."

- **Primary direction: Who Said It?** Make the sister a featured source, mixing her screenshots with the couple's messages. Let guests guess which version came from whom before she gets the final word.
- **Alternate direction: Live Trivia** Add a family-versus-friends score layer and ask questions about who noticed the relationship first, who gave advice, and who was wrong.
- **Alternate direction: The Guest Advice Oracle** Ask the sister and other early witnesses what they noticed then and what they see now. Preserve a signed archive, with sensitive answers held back from the public wall.
- **Custom angle:** Give the storyteller editorial control over one chapter, rather than flattening their story into another guest submission.

### Answer B: A friend with an embarrassing or chaotic story

> "Our friend Marcus was there the night we accidentally left a wedding in someone else's taxi. He tells it as if it was a deliberate romantic gesture."

- **Primary direction: Live Trivia** Use the disputed details as questions, with Marcus as the designated source of the final ruling. The room gets a clear laugh without handing him an unlimited microphone.
- **Alternate direction: The Story Chain** Let each table add one sentence to Marcus's version, then reveal the actual sequence and the detail he always leaves out.
- **Alternate direction: Custom Wedding Bingo** Seed the night with callbacks to the story and let guests notice when Marcus starts telling it again.
- **Custom angle:** The app should contain the storyteller's energy but also impose a boundary. A timed reveal or moderator keeps the story from taking over the reception.

### Answer C: An older relative or someone with a tender perspective

> "My aunt remembers the first Christmas he came home with us. She says he spent the whole day helping in the kitchen and never once looked at his phone."

- **Primary direction: The Two-Minute Video Guestbook** Invite the aunt to tell the story in her own voice, with an assisted recording option and a private archive of the original clip.
- **Alternate direction: Anniversary Time Capsule** Ask her to record what she hopes the couple remembers about becoming a family, then assign the message to a meaningful anniversary.
- **Alternate direction: The Guest Advice Oracle** Create a prompt for witnesses to share the first moment they saw the couple become a unit, with signed and anonymous versions of the keepsake.
- **Custom angle:** Protect the texture of the person's voice. The recommendation is about preserving a witness, not merely collecting another written memory.

## Matching Heuristics This Suggests

The answer map points to a small set of useful signals for Step 5:

- **Origin shape:** near miss, friend-introduced, online, travel, family, or work.
- **Material type:** text, voice, photo, place, object, song, person, or recurring phrase.
- **Social radius:** couple-only, one featured storyteller, a table, or the whole room.
- **Desired reveal:** laugh, argument, confession, shared creation, quiet voice, or future unlock.
- **Permission level:** public, couple-curated, private archive, or delayed until an anniversary.
- **Structure needed:** freeform story, bounded game, physical exploration, ambient collection, or private keepsake.

The strongest recommendation copy should name the answer's concrete detail and then describe the room consequence. For example: "Because your sister has the screenshots from the first date, `Who Said It?` could make her the featured witness while the room guesses which version is hers" is materially better than "You might like our guessing game."
