# Moodboard Basics

## How People Usually Do Moodboards

- Collect 10–30 images from Pinterest, Google, or Instagram into a folder or board
- Mix photos, color swatches, textures, typography samples, and real-world product shots
- Organize loosely by category (venue, florals, dress, lighting, color palette)
- Often done in Canva, Pinterest boards, Google Slides, or just a folder of screenshots
- Shared with vendors (florists, photographers, planners) as a reference brief
- Wedding planners often ask for a moodboard before the first real consultation

## Pros

- Forces couples to externalize taste — they discover what they actually want by reacting to images
- Creates shared vocabulary between couple and vendors ("moody," "airy," "garden party")
- Reduces miscommunication — one image is worth 1,000 adjectives
- Gives couples a sense of progress and excitement early in planning
- Easy to iterate — swap images, don't rewrite specs

## Cons

- Overwhelming — Pinterest rabbit holes, decision fatigue, too many tabs
- Generic results — couples find the same 40 viral wedding photos everyone else uses
- No personalization — a moodboard of floral arches says nothing about *their* story
- Disjointed — images from different sources clash in style, lighting, and color grade
- No actionable output — couples end up with a beautiful board but no clear direction

## What Makes Users Go "Wow"

- Instant visual coherence — colors and vibes feel like they belong together automatically
- Feels like it was made *for them*, not for "weddings in general"
- Seeing their own details (names, venue type, vibe words) reflected back visually
- Surprising but accurate — a suggestion they wouldn't have thought of but immediately love
- Something shareable — looks good enough to screenshot and send to their partner or planner
- Interactivity — clicking, swapping, and building feels like playing, not working

## Goals of a Moodboard Tool

- Help couples articulate their vibe before they can name it
- Surface aesthetic directions quickly without overwhelming with options
- Produce a shareable, polished artifact (not a raw dump of images)
- Create a natural hook into booking — "show this to your planner, or show it to us"
- Gather intent signals (vibe, moment type, guest count) that feed the sales funnel

## What a "Wedding Experience App Moodboard" Tool Should Do Differently

- Focus on the **guest experience**, not just flowers and table settings
- Tie vibe choices to specific app types ("moody + intimate → Love Letter Machine")
- Make the output feel like a preview of what guests will *feel*, not just what the room looks like
- Let couples pick moments ("first dance," "dinner," "after-party") and see matching app vibes
- End with a curated recommendation of 2–3 app types that fit their board — which becomes the CTA
