# Moodboard App-Led Alternatives v2

## The Question

The current moodboard asks users about:

- **Step 1:** the visual vibe of the reception;
- **Step 2:** the guest list;
- **Step 3:** the point in the day to activate;
- **Step 4:** the feeling they want afterward.

Those are useful signals, but they are indirect. A couple can select "gallery opening" and "strangers become friends" without understanding what Wepho might actually build. The moodboard then feels like a personality quiz followed by a surprise app recommendation.

The missing bridge is the **guest action**. Couples need to see whether the app asks guests to guess, vote, explore, write, record, predict, or build something together. That is what makes an existing app feel imaginable.

The goal of the alternatives below is not to turn the moodboard into a catalog. It is to introduce recognizable app directions early enough that users can say, "Yes, that is the kind of thing we want," or, "No, we would never make our guests do that."

## Shared Product Rules

Every alternative should:

- show a concrete room moment before asking for abstract preferences;
- use the 13 approved apps as recognizable starting points;
- explain the guest action in one sentence;
- show what appears in the room or what the couple receives afterward;
- let users reject an app shape without feeling that they answered incorrectly;
- avoid pretending that one tap determines the final build;
- use Step 5 answers to customize the chosen shape, not to invent an unnecessarily unusual product;
- make the final recommendation a small shortlist, ideally two or three apps, with one clear reason each.

The approved app shapes available for these flows are:

- **Room games and reveals:** `Live Trivia`, `Who Said It?`, `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`, `The Story Chain`.
- **Ambient participation:** `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `The Prediction Vault`, `The Guest Advice Oracle`, `Where To Next`, `The Home Your Room Built`.
- **Place and story exploration:** `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **Private keepsakes:** `Anniversary Time Capsule`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`.

## Alternative 1: Choose The Job

### Basic idea

Start with the job the couple wants the experience to do, rather than asking them to describe a mood. This is the clearest and most direct route from a preference to an existing app.

The user does not choose an app by name at the start. They choose a familiar wedding ambition, then see the app shapes that are good at delivering it.

### Revised flow

#### Step 1: What should this add to the reception?

Show six large cards. Each card includes a plain-English outcome and a tiny description of what guests would do.

- **Make the whole room laugh together**
  - Guests answer the same questions or guess who said what.
  - Likely directions: `Live Trivia`, `Who Said It?`.
- **Help people who do not know each other start talking**
  - Guests vote, compare answers, or explore the venue together.
  - Likely directions: `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`, `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **Tell our story without giving a long speech**
  - Guests uncover chapters or play through real moments from the relationship.
  - Likely directions: `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`, `Who Said It?`.
- **Give everyone something small to contribute**
  - Guests add a thought, prediction, place, or part of a shared creation.
  - Likely directions: `The Guest Advice Oracle`, `Where To Next`, `The Home Your Room Built`.
- **Leave us something we will still use or watch later**
  - Guests leave messages, predictions, or future recommendations.
  - Likely directions: `Anniversary Time Capsule`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`, `The Prediction Vault`.
- **Make guests notice what is happening around them**
  - Guests keep an eye out for real moments during the day.
  - Likely direction: `Custom Wedding Bingo`.

**Question copy:** `What job should the experience do? Pick the one that matters most.`

This immediately teaches the catalog without asking the couple to learn the catalog.

#### Step 2: How should guests take part?

Show five mechanic cards. This is more useful than asking only what kind of people are attending.

- **Everyone does the same thing at once**
  - Strong fit: `Live Trivia`, `Who Said It?`.
- **Guests dip in between conversations**
  - Strong fit: `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`, `Where To Next`.
- **Guests move around the venue**
  - Strong fit: `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **Guests leave a message or answer**
  - Strong fit: `The Guest Advice Oracle`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`, `Anniversary Time Capsule`.
- **Guests help build one shared result**
  - Strong fit: `The Story Chain`, `The Home Your Room Built`, `Where To Next`.

**Question copy:** `What should a guest actually do? Choose the interaction that sounds most natural for your people.`

This is the leading step the current moodboard lacks. It tells the user what an app feels like in practice.

#### Step 3: When should it happen?

Keep the existing moments step, but rewrite the supporting text around the app job:

- **Cocktail hour:** get people moving or talking;
- **Dinner:** run a short game or gather contributions between courses;
- **After dinner:** create a bounded room-wide reveal;
- **Dancing:** keep phone use extremely brief and return attention to the floor;
- **Late night:** make something for the couple to discover later.

**Question copy:** `When should this experience earn its place?`

#### Step 4: What should be left afterward?

Replace abstract feelings with a choice about the output. The emotional feeling can still be captured in copy beneath each card.

- **A story we can replay**
  - Strong fit: `Who Said It?`, `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **A list, map, or picture we can keep at home**
  - Strong fit: `Where To Next`, `The Home Your Room Built`.
- **Messages from the people we love**
  - Strong fit: `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`, `The Guest Advice Oracle`.
- **A future ritual or anniversary surprise**
  - Strong fit: `Anniversary Time Capsule`, `The Prediction Vault`.
- **A funny record of what happened on the day**
  - Strong fit: `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `Live Trivia`.
- **We mainly want the room moment; the keepsake is secondary**
  - Strong fit: `Live Trivia`, `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`.

**Question copy:** `What would you most like to have after the wedding?`

### Example outcome

> **You are leaning toward a shared reveal.** You want guests to do one thing together during dinner, and you want everyone laughing at the same time. `Live Trivia` is the clearest fit: your questions about the first date, family habits, and the stories your friends always tell become an eight-minute room event with a real winner. `Who Said It?` is the stronger alternative if you have texts, photos, or memorable lines to use.

### Why this works

- It makes the app category clear before the user reaches the results page.
- It uses language couples already understand: laugh, talk, leave messages, explore, keep.
- It produces high-value signals for matching without requiring a long story.
- It makes the difference between a live game, an ambient activity, and a private keepsake visible.

### Tradeoffs

- It is less visually dreamy than the current photo-led Step 1.
- Users who only want aesthetic inspiration may find the first question practical.
- The app directions need strong mini-examples so the cards do not look like a dry service menu.

### Best use

Use this when conversion and recommendation clarity matter more than preserving the traditional moodboard metaphor. It is the strongest option if the central problem is, "People do not understand what the apps actually are."

## Alternative 2: Keep The Moodboard, Reveal The App Underneath

### Basic idea

Keep the visual cards and emotional tone, but attach a clear app direction to every meaningful choice. The user still feels like they are building a moodboard, while the right rail quietly teaches how the answers translate into an experience.

This is the smallest change to the existing flow and the safest way to improve it without turning `/moodboard` into `/app-finder`.

### Revised Step 1: Vibe cards become scene plus outcome

Each image should have three layers:

1. the scene label;
2. a one-line description of the guest behavior;
3. a small `Often points toward` label with one or two approved apps.

Examples:

- **Dinner that got out of hand**
  - Guests answer a few questions between courses and the room sees the scoreboard.
  - Often points toward: `Live Trivia`, `Who Said It?`.
- **Film premiere energy**
  - Guests watch a sequence of reveals built around the couple's story.
  - Often points toward: `Who Said It?`, `The Story Chain`.
- **Pub quiz, best table wins**
  - Everyone plays the same short game, with a table-level winner.
  - Often points toward: `Live Trivia`.
- **Gallery opening, wine in hand**
  - Guests browse chapters, places, or messages at their own pace.
  - Often points toward: `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`, `The Guest Advice Oracle`.
- **Bonfire at the end of the night**
  - Guests leave a message that becomes a private or future keepsake.
  - Often points toward: `Anniversary Time Capsule`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`.
- **Rooftop, city below**
  - Guests add places and future plans to a shared map.
  - Often points toward: `Where To Next`.
- **Brunch that never ended**
  - Guests add small observations throughout the day and the couple gets the record afterward.
  - Often points toward: `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `The Guest Advice Oracle`.
- **Everyone ended up in the kitchen**
  - Guests contribute to one shared result while still talking at their tables.
  - Often points toward: `The Home Your Room Built`, `The Story Chain`.

**Question copy:** `Which scene feels most like the kind of reception you want?`

The key is that the app label is secondary. The user chooses the feeling first, but never has to wonder what that feeling might become.

### Revised Step 2: Guest cards become guest behavior cards

Keep the familiar guest descriptions, but add a consequence line beneath each one.

- **Grandparents in the front row**
  - Favor voice, simple prompts, and assisted recording.
  - Likely directions: `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`, `Anniversary Time Capsule`.
- **Half the room never met**
  - Favor low-pressure reasons to compare answers or explore together.
  - Likely directions: `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`, `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **Wild college friends**
  - Favor a clear host, a bounded game, and content that can be moderated.
  - Likely directions: `Live Trivia`, `Who Said It?`, `The Story Chain`.
- **Lots of kids**
  - Favor short, observable actions with an adult fallback.
  - Likely directions: `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `The Home Your Room Built`.
- **Work crowd mixed in**
  - Give newer guests an easy way into the couple's story.
  - Likely directions: `Who Said It?`, `Live Trivia`.
- **Reserved but warm**
  - Favor anonymous, ambient, or private participation rather than microphone moments.
  - Likely directions: `The Guest Advice Oracle`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`.

**Question copy:** `Which description sounds most like your guests? What kind of participation would feel comfortable for them?`

The second sentence matters. It teaches that the guest list changes the mechanic, not just the visual theme.

### Keep Step 3, but make the preview temporal

The current moments step is useful. The improvement is to show a small example of an app in that moment:

- **Cocktail hour:** `Guests scan a chapter at the bar, then walk to the garden for the next one.`
- **Dinner:** `The screen shows one question between courses; everyone answers, then the room sees the result.`
- **After dinner:** `The MC opens the final reveal and the room sees what its contributions made.`
- **Dancing:** `A guest taps once, the DJ gets the result, and the phone goes away.`
- **Late night:** `The last guests leave predictions for the couple to open later.`

### Revised Step 4: Feelings become feeling plus mechanism

Keep emotional language, but add an explanation of the kind of app that usually delivers it.

- **Everyone laughing at the same time**
  - Usually comes from a short room game or reveal.
  - Likely directions: `Live Trivia`, `Who Said It?`.
- **Strangers became friends**
  - Usually comes from a prompt that gives people a reason to compare answers.
  - Likely directions: `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`, `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **A keepsake from everyone**
  - Usually comes from low-pressure contributions collected over the night.
  - Likely directions: `The Guest Advice Oracle`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`.
- **Our story was the main character**
  - Usually comes from chronology, real messages, or meaningful places.
  - Likely directions: `Who Said It?`, `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **Guests actually looked up**
  - Usually comes from brief phone actions followed by a visible room payoff.
  - Likely directions: `Live Trivia`, `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `The Story Chain`.
- **Something nobody has seen before**
  - First show the closest approved shape, then offer a custom twist. Do not jump straight to a completely new app.
  - Likely directions: any approved app with a couple-specific format change.

**Question copy:** `What do you want the room to feel, and what should guests be doing to create it?`

### Example outcome

> **Your board is pointing toward something social and story-led.** The dinner-party and film-premiere choices suggest a shared reveal, while your mixed guest list means newer friends need a way into the story. We would start with `Who Said It?`: a short sequence of real messages and photos, with guests guessing who said each one. If you want less screen focus and more movement, `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story` is the better direction.

### Why this works

- It preserves the existing visual identity and most of the current questions.
- It makes each answer legible as a recommendation signal.
- The user sees a live explanation instead of waiting for a mysterious final match.
- It is easy to implement with hand-authored relationships between cards and apps.

### Tradeoffs

- The cards may become text-heavy if the app explanation is not tightly designed.
- Users could anchor too early on the app labels and stop exploring.
- Some vibe-to-app connections are naturally weak, so the copy must say `often points toward`, not `this means you should choose`.

### Best use

Use this when the team wants to improve the current product without replacing its moodboard character. It is the best low-risk first release and probably the best default direction.

## Alternative 3: App Tasting Menu

### Basic idea

Let users briefly try the types of interaction Wepho builds before asking them to describe their own wedding. The first step is not a questionnaire. It is a set of tiny, polished previews showing what a guest might actually do.

The user chooses the experiences that feel natural, exciting, or appropriate. Then Steps 2 and 4 help determine who can participate and what the outcome should be.

This is the most fun and the most persuasive option, but it requires more product work than the other two.

### Revised Step 1: Taste six guest actions

Show six interactive cards. Each has a 5- to 15-second simulated interaction and a plain-English explanation.

- **Answer together**
  - Tap an answer to a couple question and watch the room leaderboard update.
  - App directions: `Live Trivia`.
- **Guess who said it**
  - Tap `Partner A` or `Partner B` on a blurred message, then reveal the sender.
  - App directions: `Who Said It?`.
- **Explore the story**
  - Tap a venue point to unlock a chapter of a relationship.
  - App directions: `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story`.
- **Leave something for later**
  - Record or write a short message, choose an anniversary, and see it seal.
  - App directions: `Anniversary Time Capsule`, `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`.
- **Add to something shared**
  - Add one place, home detail, or sentence and watch the collective result grow.
  - App directions: `Where To Next`, `The Home Your Room Built`, `The Story Chain`.
- **Take a side**
  - Vote on a harmless couple opinion and see the room split.
  - App directions: `The Couple's Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker`.

**Question copy:** `Which of these would your guests actually enjoy doing? Pick up to two.`

Each card should end with a small line: `The phone is the input. The room is the payoff.` This communicates the product model more effectively than a feature list.

### Revised Step 2: Choose the right guest version

Once the user chooses one or two actions, ask how their guests would participate:

- **Everyone together for 5-10 minutes**
  - Keeps `Live Trivia` and `Who Said It?` strong.
- **Small moments throughout the reception**
  - Keeps `Custom Wedding Bingo`, `The Guest Advice Oracle`, and `Where To Next` strong.
- **A little movement and discovery**
  - Keeps `Venue Scavenger Hunt with Their Love Story` strong.
- **A quieter experience people can do in their own time**
  - Keeps `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook`, `Anniversary Time Capsule`, and `The Prediction Vault` strong.
- **A shared result everyone can watch grow**
  - Keeps `The Story Chain` and `The Home Your Room Built` strong.

**Question copy:** `Which version would suit your guests best?`

This is more actionable than asking whether the guest list is "loud" or "reserved". Those descriptors can remain as secondary signals.

### Keep Step 3 as the planning check

After the user has tasted an interaction, ask when it belongs:

**Question copy:** `When would this feel most natural?`

Use the existing timeline cards, but make the selected tasting card appear inside the moment:

- `Who Said It? during dinner`
- `Venue Scavenger Hunt during cocktail hour`
- `Video Guestbook throughout the day`
- `The Home Your Room Built between courses`

This makes the timeline feel like planning an experience instead of filling in a form.

### Revised Step 4: Choose the output

Ask what the couple wants the experience to produce:

- **A live room moment**
  - The room watches a reveal, scoreboard, or shared result.
- **A conversation between guests**
  - The mechanic gives people a reason to talk to someone nearby.
- **A personal archive**
  - The couple receives the actual messages, answers, or recordings.
- **A future anniversary moment**
  - The content is sealed and delivered later.
- **A physical object for the home**
  - The result becomes a map, poster, book, or chapbook.

**Question copy:** `What should this become after the reception?`

### Step 5 becomes customization, not discovery

This approach makes Step 5 much easier. The couple already understands the app direction, so the story questions can ask for only the material needed to make that direction theirs.

If they picked `Who Said It?` or `Live Trivia`, ask:

- `What part of your story would your guests enjoy guessing about?`
- `Who knows one side of the story better than anyone else?`

If they picked `Video Guestbook` or `Anniversary Time Capsule`, ask:

- `Who do you most want to hear from years from now?`
- `What would you like guests to remember about your life together?`

If they picked `Where To Next` or `The Home Your Room Built`, ask:

- `What are you excited to do, see, or build together next?`
- `What places, objects, pets, or routines already feel like home?`

If they picked `Venue Scavenger Hunt`, ask:

- `Which places at the venue could carry a real part of your story?`
- `What is the final thing guests should discover?`

This is a better use of Step 5 than asking every couple for eight open-ended anecdotes regardless of the app direction.

### Example outcome

> **You liked guessing and leaving something behind.** Your guests can handle a short shared game, but you also want voices and messages you can keep. We would recommend `Who Said It?` for the live dinner moment, using a small set of approved texts or quotes, with `The Two-Minute Video Guestbook` as the quieter keepsake layer. The two apps solve different jobs instead of forcing one experience to do everything.

### Why this works

- Couples understand the product by experiencing it.
- The app recommendation feels earned because the user selected the behavior, not just a mood.
- It naturally supports combinations, such as a room game plus a private keepsake.
- It reduces the burden on Step 5 because the questions become relevant to the chosen direction.

### Tradeoffs

- It requires six polished mini-previews and more interaction design.
- A couple could choose based on the demo rather than their actual wedding constraints.
- The tasting flow must be honest that these are starting points, not the finished app.
- It may make the tool feel more like a product configurator than a moodboard.

### Best use

Use this when the moodboard is a major sales tool and the team is willing to invest in showing the quality of the experiences. It is the strongest long-term direction for helping couples imagine why a custom wedding app is worth paying for.

## Comparison

| Alternative | What the user chooses first | How clearly it leads to existing apps | Fun factor | Build effort | Main risk |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Choose The Job | The outcome they want | High | Medium | Low to medium | Feels like a service selector |
| 2. Reveal The App Underneath | A visual mood and guest context | Medium to high | High | Low | Can become text-heavy or anchor too early |
| 3. App Tasting Menu | The guest action they enjoyed | Very high | Very high | High | Feels less like a traditional moodboard |

## Recommendation

Use **Alternative 2** as the immediate direction, with one important addition from Alternative 3: every early card should show a tiny example of the guest action and room outcome.

That gives the product a practical progression:

1. **Step 1:** choose a reception scene, and see the app directions it commonly supports;
2. **Step 2:** choose the guest reality, and see which mechanics are comfortable or credible;
3. **Step 3:** choose when the experience belongs in the timeline;
4. **Step 4:** choose the desired feeling, paired with the mechanism likely to create it;
5. **Step 5:** provide only the personal material needed to customize the surviving app directions;
6. **Step 6:** choose the final visual energy and review the shortlist.

The first release does not need six fully playable demos. It can use short animated examples, a phone frame, or one sentence describing the room moment. Later, the two best-performing directions can become real mini-tastes.

The matching language should move from:

> `Your answers suggest a creative, social, story-led experience.`

to:

> `You want guests to do something together during dinner, and your story has enough real material for a guessing game. We would start with Live Trivia or Who Said It? Here is what guests would actually do, and what the room would see.`

That is the bridge between a moodboard answer and a believable app recommendation.
