 # 10 Most Suitable Apps — Romantic & Classical Wedding

Selection criteria: couples who describe their wedding as **romantic and classical** — think candlelit dinner, string quartet during cocktails, black-tie or garden-formal, sincere speeches, first dance, meaningful traditions. The primary emotional register is *tender, timeless, keepsake-first* rather than *funny, chaotic, competitive*.

Filter applied against `one-pager.md` and `app-guidelines.md`:

- Only apps from the **13 approved concepts** in `app-guidelines.md` are eligible.
- Prioritize the guidelines' **"Private keepsakes"** and **"Ambient participation"** shapes; deprioritize loud "Room games and reveals" that would break a candlelit register.
- Every pick must still respect the Five Rules from `one-pager.md` (phone-in/phone-out ≤ 60s, room is the output, couple curates, one-couple-one-night, keepsake output).
- Excluded for tonal mismatch: **Custom Wedding Bingo** (funny/whimsical), **Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker** (playful debate/conflict), **The Story Chain** (chaotic, comedic drift).

---

## The Shortlist (ranked by fit)

### 1. Anniversary Time Capsule
The single most romantic app in the catalog. Guests record short messages sealed to a future anniversary — the wedding day becomes a ritual that keeps giving on year 1, 5, 10. Cocktail-hour or dinner moment, quiet, dignified, no spectacle required.
**Why it fits:** Private-keepsake shape. Turns guests' presence into a future love-letter delivery. Zero comedy pressure.

### 2. The Unprompted Love Letter Machine
The studio's flagship emotional demo and the most tonally romantic experience in the catalog. Guests type a short message; the couple reads them live from the big screen during speeches. Grandma's message on the wall is the archetypal romantic-classical moment. Now part of the approved concepts in `app-guidelines.md`.
**Why it fits:** The one-pager positions this as *the* "make them cry" demo. If the couple wants romance, this is the closest to a default answer.

### 3. The Two-Minute Video Guestbook
The classical guestbook, upgraded. No booth queue, no attendant — guests record from their seat during a natural pause. Ends as a curated highlight film plus individual clips. Preserves the voices of older and distant relatives, which matters most in a traditional register.
**Why it fits:** Private-keepsake shape. Direct heir to the sign-in book tradition. No public wall required.

### 4. The Guest Advice Oracle
The classical marriage-advice tradition made searchable and revisitable. Prompts are sincere ("what should they remember when the thermostat argument starts in year three?") rather than jokey. Delivered as a private archive plus optional printed edition — the keepsake feels like a family heirloom.
**Why it fits:** Ambient participation, sincere register, keepsake-first. Elevates a beloved tradition without mocking it.

### 5. Relationship Origin Story Exhibit *(from the 20, currently non-live)*
Guests move through the couple's chronology like a small museum during cocktail hour — photos, artifacts, short captions at each station. Explicitly tagged "Romantic / Emotional" in the one-pager table.
**Why it fits:** Place-and-story-exploration shape. Fits a string-quartet cocktail hour perfectly; encourages the kind of quiet, informed conversation classical weddings are built around.

### 6. Venue Scavenger Hunt With Their Love Story
A sequence of chapters tied to real venue locations — the bar where they met the parents, the staircase, the garden bench. Not a race, not a puzzle; a paced walk through the couple's story.
**Why it fits:** Cocktail-hour movement, story-driven, uses the venue as the interface. Works beautifully at a formal, architecturally interesting venue (chateau, estate, historic hotel).

### 7. Where To Next
Guests pin places to visit, live, retire — each with a short reason. Ends as a framed printed map. Sincere register by default; nothing forces jokes.
**Why it fits:** Ambient, low-pressure, produces a hangable keepsake that belongs on the couple's wall — a very classical outcome.

### 8. The Prediction Vault
Sealed predictions unsealed on future anniversaries. Can absolutely lean romantic (what will they still argue about, what will they have learned, which friend called it) if the couple keeps the prompts warm rather than jokey.
**Why it fits:** Private-keepsake shape, future-ritual promise. Same emotional physics as the time capsule with a lighter cadence.

### 9. Live "How Well Do You Know Us?" Trivia
The most romantic room-game in the catalog when the questions are chosen well. "Who proposed first and what did she say" beats "what's his middle name." Kept to eight minutes, held at dinner, with a toast as the payoff — this fits a classical reception rhythm.
**Why it fits:** The only "Room games and reveals" pick, included because it can be run in a dignified register and produces a genuine communal moment during dinner.

### 10. Ask Us Anything
Guests submit and upvote questions; the couple answers the top few at the mic. In a classical setting this reads as an elegant Q&A moment (think: dinner-party toast tradition) rather than a talk show. Depends heavily on tone — the couple must keep it sincere and moderate hard.
**Why it fits:** Speeches-adjacent, mic-driven, works with a hosted dinner structure. Fragile — swap it for the Video Guestbook if the couple is at all shy of the spotlight.

## What we did not pick, and why

| App | Why excluded |
|---|---|
| Custom Wedding Bingo | Comedic register; asks guests to spot chaos, which cuts against a candlelit tone. |
| Unpopular Opinions Icebreaker | Built around affectionate disagreement — wrong emotional temperature for a classical wedding. |
| The Story Chain | Chaos is a feature; readable romance is not the reliable outcome. |
| The Home Your Room Built | Charming but leans playful/illustrated; sits closer to whimsy than classical romance. |

*The Home Your Room Built is a defensible eleventh pick if the couple wants a lighter beat between dinner courses.*
