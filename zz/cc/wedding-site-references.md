# Wedding-adjacent site references worth stealing from

Five sites past the obvious tier (Zola / The Knot / Joy / Minted / Squarespace-template planners). All are boutique planner or wedding-photography studios — a closer analogue to Wepho than any of the mass-market platforms, because they sell taste and a one-time service, not a subscription.

Use these as reference targets in the sense described in `designing-full-apps-with-cc.md`: not "make it look like this," but "the *reason* this feels bespoke is X, do X."

---

## 1. Easton Events — `eastonevents.com`

**One-liner:** Charleston-based destination planner, ~10 events/year worldwide, positions as "quiet luxury."

**Why it works:**
- Reads like a luxury magazine, not a service page. The homepage is a single photograph at a time, with generous whitespace framing a tight column of small-caps sans and a serif display face.
- Restrained palette: warm bone, ink, one muted terracotta. No accent color used for CTAs — the CTA is a hairline-underlined text link, which quietly signals "we don't need to shout."
- Typography carries the personality — a high-contrast serif at 60–80px paired with a spaced small-caps sans at ~11px. The size *difference* is the design.
- Case studies open to full-bleed photography with editorial captions rather than "highlights of the day" copy.

**Steal:** the hairline-underline CTA treatment, the small-caps-sans + display-serif contrast, the discipline of never using a card grid.

---

## 2. La Fête — `la-fete.com`

**One-liner:** New York luxury wedding & events designer.

**Why it works:**
- Full-bleed motion on the hero (slow crossfades between photographs, not a video reel) — establishes atmosphere before a single word of copy.
- Navigation is text-only, uppercase, letter-spaced, and thin. It disappears into the layout instead of hovering as a chrome bar.
- Sections don't announce themselves with H2s — they use oversized numerals ("01", "02") or a single italic word as the label. Feels curated, not templated.
- Color comes almost entirely from the photographs; the site UI is nearly monochrome.

**Steal:** oversized-numeral section labels, letter-spaced uppercase nav, and the discipline of letting the imagery be the color.

---

## 3. Jesse Tombs — `jessetombs.com` (Alison Events / Jesse Tombs)

**One-liner:** San Francisco full-service event design studio, "editorial yet deeply human."

**Why it works:**
- Editorial without being cold. Copy is written in first person and uses specific proper nouns ("the olive grove in Sonoma," "grandmother's linens") — the exact specificity move Wepho is trying to make.
- Layouts are asymmetric. Photographs are placed off-grid, occasionally rotated a degree or two, with handwritten-style captions. Feels like a mood board, not a case-study slider.
- Uses two serif faces (one for display, one for body) — an unusual choice that reads more like a print magazine than a website.

**Steal:** first-person voice + proper-noun specificity in body copy, off-grid photo placement, dual-serif type system.

---

## 4. Boutique Weddings Prague — `boutiqueweddings.cz`

**One-liner:** Prague-based destination planner. Awwwards Honorable Mention.

**Why it works:**
- Storytelling-first structure — the site is built like a narrative, with unusual navigation (horizontal scroll through the service journey rather than a stacked page).
- Uses full-screen video as texture — soft, slow shots of hands, glassware, candlelight — not as a big hero pitch reel. Sets a mood without a voiceover.
- Custom cursor + subtle scroll-driven transitions. Feels crafted rather than templated. Motion is used to punctuate, not decorate.
- Type is a large modern serif at scale, italic used often, no icon anywhere on the page.

**Steal:** motion as punctuation (not decoration), video-as-texture, absence of icons — nothing on the page needs an icon to explain what it is.

---

## 5. OBSESD Wedding Photography — `obsesd.com`

**One-liner:** Portland wedding photographer. Awwwards Honorable Mention.

**Why it works:**
- Photography sites are the closest analogue to Wepho — high-emotion, one-time service, sold on taste. This one is especially disciplined.
- Interactive gallery treats each shoot as its own micro-site, with a distinct color pulled from the imagery. The system flexes per-project instead of forcing every case study into the same template.
- The "about" page is a long-form personal essay in a single narrow column of body serif — no bullet list of services, no headshot grid. Reads like a Substack post, not a bio.
- Zero stock UI. No card shadows, no gradient buttons, no rounded avatars. The chrome is nearly invisible.

**Steal:** per-project color extraction, long-form single-column "about" essay, the ruthless removal of default UI chrome.

## Cross-cutting patterns

If four of the five sites do a thing, that thing is probably load-bearing for the aesthetic and is what Wepho is currently missing:

1. **No card grids.** None of these sites uses the "3 columns of feature cards" pattern. Wepho leans on it heavily.
2. **Type contrast is the design.** Big display serif + tiny letter-spaced sans. Not two mid-weight sans doing everything.
3. **The CTA whispers.** Hairline underline, small-caps sans, no filled pill. Confidence signal.
4. **Photography is the color system.** UI palette is 2–3 neutrals; every hit of color comes from the imagery.
5. **Sections don't announce themselves.** No `<h2>Features</h2>`. Section identity comes from a numeral, an italic word, or the copy itself.

Any one of these applied to Wepho would move it away from generic. All five applied would make it feel like it belongs in this reference set.
