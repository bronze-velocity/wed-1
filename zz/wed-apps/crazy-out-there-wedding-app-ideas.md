# 20 Crazy, Out-There Wedding App Ideas

Partner doc to `real-world-interactive-wedding-examples.md`. Where that file catalogs what's *already* being run at weddings (Kahoot, photo walls, QR song requests), this file goes the other direction: 20 speculative concepts that essentially no one is doing yet, but which still respect the Wepho positioning.

Every idea below satisfies the one-pager's Five Rules: **phone in / phone out in under 60 seconds**, **magic in the room, not on the screen**, **couple curates before anything goes public**, **built for one couple, one night**, **no code touched by the couple**. All are QR → browser only, all feed the big screen or the physical venue, all ship a keepsake output.

*Drafted 2026-08-21. Provocations, not backlog items.*

---

## Cocktail Hour / Arrival

### 1. The Guest Constellation
Each guest scans a QR at the entrance and answers three prompts: *when* they met the couple (year), *where* (city / context), and one shared memory in ≤ 8 words. A live star map builds on the venue projector — each guest becomes a star placed on a timeline-map, connected by shared eras (college, first job, the dog rescue group). By dinner, the room's shared history is a physical constellation the couple walks their parents through.
*Why it fits Wepho: extreme specificity (their real timeline), 30-second phone use, output is a print-ready poster the couple frames.*

### 2. The Living Portrait Mosaic
Guests upload one selfie on arrival. A large projected canvas begins as a blurred silhouette of the couple; as more selfies land, the AI-composed mosaic sharpens into a recognizable portrait made of hundreds of guest faces. The image is only fully resolved once ~80% of the room has contributed — turning "did you upload yet?" into peer pressure that fills itself.
*Why it fits: participation loop is visible on the wall; keepsake is the final composite.*

### 3. The Personalized Perfume Vote
Guests vote on scent notes ("smoky? citrus? something that smells like the forest behind her parents' house?") on their phones during cocktail hour. A perfumer or bartender behind the bar actually mixes the winning combination in real time — the couple's signature scent for the night, bottled in small vials as favors on the way out.
*Why it fits: room-first, keepsake ships with guests, deeply "us."*

### 4. AR Ghost Tour of the Couple
Guests point their phones at specific spots in the venue (marked by small numbered plaques) and see AR overlays of the couple's actual history — a photo of their first apartment overlaid on the bar wall, a voice note from him about their third date pinned to a doorway, a text screenshot ("I think I love you") floating over the courtyard bench. Unlock each spot by "visiting" it.
*Why it fits: private history mapped onto the physical room; browser AR, no app install.*

## Dinner

### 5. The Live Fairy Tale Machine
Every guest contributes one sentence to a bedtime-story-style fairy tale about the couple ("Once upon a time, in a town where the buses never came on time…"). Couple approves in the moderation queue. At dessert, the emcee reads the stitched fairy tale aloud from the big screen — the room hears a story it wrote about the couple, in the couple's voice.
*Why it fits: couple curates, room is the payoff, PDF keepsake at the end.*

### 6. Table-vs-Table Debate League
Each table is randomly assigned a side of a "how well do you know the couple" claim ("Table 4: defend the position that Sam is the messier one"). Tables have 90 seconds to submit a joint argument; the couple picks a winner per round. Winning table gets a real, physical perk — first dessert served, dance-floor priority, a bottle of wine sent over.
*Why it fits: creates cross-table energy strangers can't ignore; physical prize keeps the room engaged.*

### 7. The Reverse Piñata
A meter on the big screen fills as guests submit wishes/predictions/burns from their phones. Every time the meter hits a threshold, a real thing happens in the venue: sparklers get handed out, the dessert cart rolls in early, a surprise pre-recorded video from an absent friend plays, the band starts an unannounced encore. The room learns fast that participation triggers physical delight.
*Why it fits: phone is input, venue is output, at its purest.*

### 8. The Prediction Vault (Sealed for 25 Years)
Guests write dated predictions about the couple's next 1, 5, 10, and 25 years — anything from "you'll move back to Portland by 2031" to "you'll finally get a dog and name it after your grandpa." Sealed after the wedding. On each anniversary, the couple gets an email opening only that year's predictions with attribution to who wrote each one.
*Why it fits: keepsake extends the app's life for decades; couple curates what gets sealed.*

### 9. Deep Vow Remix
The couple's real vows appear on screen line by line. Guests get one line to "remix" — write a funnier, sadder, or more honest alternate version of that specific vow line. Best remixes (couple-approved) are projected next to the original. Ends up as a printed dual-column keepsake: the vows they wrote, and the vows their friends wish they'd written.
*Why it fits: intimate source material, tightly moderated, printable output.*

### 10. Living Genealogy Draft
Guests draft each other into fictional future roles in the couple's life: "godparent of first kid," "person they call at 3am in a crisis," "who officiates the vow renewal in 2051," "who they'll get stuck in Europe with." Live bracket forms on screen. Winners find out publicly and can accept or decline on their phone (with a scripted "acceptance speech" field).
*Why it fits: creates future-tense in-jokes the room owns forever.*

## Speeches

### 11. The Deep-Fake Toast
Ahead of the reception (via a QR code sent with the invite), every guest records themselves saying one assigned line of a scripted toast — 8–12 seconds each. On the night, the lines stitch into one seamless mega-toast played on the big screen: 100 voices delivering one continuous speech about the couple, cutting between faces every few words.
*Why it fits: participation happens pre-event so speech-night flow is unbroken; the video is the keepsake.*

### 12. The Anonymous Confession Wall
Guests submit anonymous confessions relating to the couple: "I lost your engagement ring for two hours in 2019 and never told you." "The reason I couldn't make it to your engagement party is I was on a Bumble date with your cousin." Couple pre-approves every confession before the wall shows it during the speech block. Ranges from tender to devastating; entirely couple-controlled.
*Why it fits: extreme moderation model, room-first payoff, unforgettable.*

### 13. The Parallel Universe Vows
Guests draft one line of vows from a parallel-universe version of the couple ("In the universe where you moved to Berlin instead," "In the universe where you never swiped right"). Best 10 approved lines are stitched into a "vow renewal from another timeline" that the emcee reads aloud between real toasts.
*Why it fits: canonical Wepho move — specificity built from private history.*

## Dancing / Late Night

### 14. Three-Channel Silent Disco Vote
The venue runs three DJ streams simultaneously through disposable QR-code headphones handed out at the door. Live vote counts on the projector show which channel is winning the room. The losing channel gets swapped out every 20 minutes by crowd vote — the DJ set literally curated in real time by the guests still standing.
*Why it fits: room-first, phone is minimal input, no one is scrolling.*

### 15. Choose-Your-Own-Adventure First Dance
The couple has rehearsed 4 short choreographed sequences (a dip, a lift, a comedy bit, a slow-sway). Screen prompts appear at inflection points during the song; guests vote which sequence comes next. The couple performs whatever the room chose — every wedding they've been to did the same first dance; this one, the guests choreographed live.
*Why it fits: room co-authors the moment; recording of chosen version is the keepsake.*

### 16. Room-Wide Mood Ring
Guests rate the vibe on their phone every 20 minutes with a single tap (sad / warm / hyped / unhinged). Color-changing floral installations, the uplighting, and even the ceiling projection subtly shift to reflect the room's collective emotional state. By 11pm, the walls are pulsing amber-red because everyone said "unhinged." Post-event: an emotion graph of the whole night.
*Why it fits: physical venue is the output surface; graph is the keepsake.*

### 17. The Live Cover-Song Rewrite
A well-known song (couple's karaoke go-to, or the song they danced to on their third date) is chosen. Guests suggest one lyric substitution each ("change 'walking on sunshine' to 'walking to Trader Joe's'"). Couple picks the funniest 12 substitutions. A live musician performs the rewritten version at midnight — the crowd is hearing the song they wrote together, once.
*Why it fits: room co-writes, live performance is the payoff, MP3 is the keepsake.*

## After the Wedding

### 18. Time-Traveling Guest Reactions
Guests record a 10-second video reaction *as if it were 10 years from now*, looking back on tonight ("Oh my god, that wedding. I still think about the way…"). Nothing is shown on the night. On the 10th anniversary, the couple gets one email with all the videos auto-compiled — the room's future selves talking about a night that just happened.
*Why it fits: sealed keepsake, no distraction from the actual night, deeply "us."*

### 19. Guest-Powered Vow Renewal Script
Across the night, guests contribute lines to a vow renewal script — one line each, prompted by categories (a promise, a warning, a memory, a wish). The couple never sees it. On their 25th anniversary, the finished script is delivered as a bound booklet, so that when they renew their vows, the words are literally written by everyone who was there the first night.
*Why it fits: makes the app matter for a quarter century; couple curates the source prompts.*

### 20. The Wedding Simulator
A 60-second mini-game where each guest plays as "young the couple" on their real first date — dialogue choices, small quick-time events, an ending screen showing their "date score." Leaderboard on the big screen ranks who best re-lived the night. Post-event, the couple gets a browsable web page of every guest's playthrough transcript, essentially 150 different attempts to imagine being them.
*Why it fits: couple-authored dialogue is the entire game; leaderboard drives the room; browsable transcripts are a bizarrely intimate keepsake.*

## What These Have in Common (and Why That Matters for Positioning)

- **None are runnable with off-the-shelf tools.** Kahoot can't do a mosaic that sharpens with participation. Mentimeter can't stitch a fairy tale. Every idea here requires a bespoke build — which is exactly the "custom studio, not template" wedge Wepho occupies.
- **Most extend past the night.** Sealed prophecies, 10-year reaction reels, 25th-anniversary vow booklets — the wildest ideas are also the ones that make the $2,000 look small because the couple keeps unwrapping them for decades.
- **Every one still respects the Five Rules.** Even the ones that sound out of control (deep-fake toast, three-channel silent disco, projected confessions) route through couple approval, run in a browser, and put the payoff in the room.
- **They point toward a "brainstorm anything" pitch.** These 20 are proof, on paper, that Rule 4 ("the couple can come up with any idea, however weird") is real. This doc is a partner artifact for planner / couple conversations that need to see the ceiling, not just the floor.
