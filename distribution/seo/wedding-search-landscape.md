# Wedding Search Landscape

How brides, grooms, and planners search Google in 2026 — and where the openings are.

> **Product-fit note.** This doc surveys the whole wedding search market. For a bespoke wedding **reception app** service (live trivia, roasts, icebreakers, story exhibits), the relevant clusters are #7 (RSVP-adjacent tools are NOT you), the vendor/entertainment tail below, and — crucially — a category not enumerated here: reception games / entertainment / activities. See `strategy.md` for the reception-app-specific keyword set ("wedding reception games", "photo booth alternatives", "Hochzeitsspiele", etc.). Ignore the venues/dresses/registry/planning-tool clusters; they're not your buyer's search intent.

> All volumes and rankings below reflect training-data knowledge through early 2026. Re-verify with a live keyword tool (DataForSEO Labs, Ahrefs, Semrush) before committing budget.

---

## Highest-volume category clusters

Ranked roughly by US monthly search volume:

1. **Venues** — "wedding venues near me", "[city] wedding venues", "barn/outdoor/beach wedding venues". The single biggest commercial cluster; The Knot / WeddingWire dominate.
2. **Dresses & attire** — retail-owned (David's Bridal, Azazie, BHLDN).
3. **Planning umbrella** — "wedding planning", "wedding checklist", "how to plan a wedding".
4. **Wedding website** — mid-hundreds-of-thousands range; Zola, The Knot, Joy own it.
5. **Invitations & save-the-dates** — Minted, Zola, Etsy, Canva.
6. **Budget** — "wedding budget calculator", "average wedding cost".
7. **RSVP** — "online RSVP", "RSVP tracker".
8. **Seating charts** — smaller but very tool-intent.
9. **Guest list** — often merged into website builders.
10. **Registry** — Zola and Amazon dominate.
11. **Vendors** — photographers, florists, DJs, caterers; long tail geographically.
12. **Timelines / day-of** — rising with AI-assisted planning.

---

## Interactive tool intent (where the app fits)

Highest search demand for tools people actually want to use:

- **Wedding website builder** — biggest by far, high commercial intent, saturated.
- **Budget calculator** — high volume, mostly informational, low-friction lead capture.
- **Seating chart maker** — mid volume, very high intent; AllSeated, Prismm, WeddingWire's tool are the incumbents.
- **RSVP tracker / online RSVP**.
- **Guest list manager** — often bundled with website builders.
- **Timeline generator / day-of scheduler** — rising fast.
- **Registry** — dominated by Zola/Amazon; hard to enter.

## Modifiers that matter

- **"free"** — huge multiplier on tool searches. Non-negotiable to rank for.
- **"template"** — Canva and Etsy own it.
- **"app"** — smaller than "website" but rising; often mobile-store intent.
- **"online"** — pairs with RSVP, invitations, planner.
- **"AI"** — fast-growing modifier in 2025-26 ("AI wedding planner", "AI seating chart", "AI vow generator"). Low competition, high novelty.
- **"generator"** — vows, speeches, hashtags, timelines. Cheap tools rank easily.
- **"for couples"** — softer, inclusive framing; helpful for same-sex-friendly positioning.
- **"2026" / "2027"** — trend, color, inspiration queries spike with year modifiers each fall.

## Long-tail / question queries

Most content-SEO traffic comes from "how" and "what" queries:

- "how to plan a wedding in 6 months / on a budget / step by step"
- "what to include in a wedding website"
- "how many bridesmaids"
- "when to send save the dates"
- "best wedding websites free"
- "how to make a seating chart"
- "wedding hashtag ideas / generator"

Warning: these are the queries AI Overviews eats fastest. Expect 20–40% CTR decay on pure informational wedding queries. Plan for GEO alongside SEO — see `../geo/`.

## Seasonality

- **Engagement season**: late-November through Valentine's Day. ~40% of US proposals happen Dec-Feb. Planning-related search ("wedding website", "wedding checklist", "wedding planning") spikes hardest **January-February**.
- **Wedding season**: May-October (US peak Sep-Oct). Day-of tool searches (seating charts, timelines, hashtag generators) peak **April-August**.
- **RSVP / seating chart** peaks lag 2-3 months before the wedding-season peak.
- **DE market**: engagements skew Christmas/NYE; weddings concentrate May-September with a strong July-August peak.

## Competitor landscape

- **The Knot (theknot.com)** — SEO leader for venues, vendors, planning content. Programmatic city pages. Owns WeddingWire (same parent: Knot Worldwide).
- **Zola** — dominates "wedding website" and "registry" head terms. Product-led SEO, high DA.
- **Joy (withjoy.com)** — strong on "free wedding website"; modern UX, growing organic.
- **Minted** — invitations/save-the-dates; brand + product SEO.
- **Appy Couple / Riley & Grey** — premium/design-forward, mostly brand traffic, small organic footprint.
- Long-tail tool niches (seating, hashtag, vow generators) are still winnable by solo builders — the big players under-invest in single-purpose tools.

---

## Regional notes (relevant for DE market)

- **US** — largest market, English, most competitive. Programmatic geo pages work.
- **UK** — Hitched.co.uk dominates; smaller volumes, similar patterns.
- **DE** — much smaller search volumes but weaker SEO competition. Key terms:
  - Hochzeitshomepage, Hochzeitswebsite
  - Sitzplan Hochzeit
  - Hochzeitsplaner
  - Save the Date Vorlage
  - Gästeliste Hochzeit
  - Main competitors: **weddyplace.com, hochzeitsplaza.de, weddix.de, eweddy.com**.
  - Zola/Joy are not localized for DE — real opening.
  - German couples lean more on Pinterest and Instagram than US couples; SEO alone under-serves the funnel — pair with `../pins/`.
- Consider bilingual (DE + EN) from day one for DACH — many international/expat couples in Berlin/Munich search in English.
