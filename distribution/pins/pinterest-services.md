# Distributing Services on Pinterest

Pinterest is a search engine, not a social network. That reframing changes everything about how it works for service businesses.

---

## Why it works for services

- 96% of top searches are unbranded — people search for solutions, not brands. Small accounts can compete with big ones.
- Content lifespan is measured in years, not hours. A pin from 2 years ago can still drive leads today.
- High purchase intent. Users come to plan and decide, not scroll passively.
- Accounts with few followers can outperform large ones because reach is driven by keyword relevance, not audience size.

---

## The core funnel

Pinterest → blog post / freebie → email list → offer

Don't send pins straight to a sales or booking page. Pinterest users are usually new to you and need value first. Route them to a useful piece of content (blog post, guide, checklist) that has your opt-in or CTA embedded.

---

## Content that works for service providers

- **Tips and how-tos** — position you as the expert, get saved and shared
- **Frameworks and processes** — works well for coaches, consultants, strategists
- **Before/after transformations** — e.g. web designers, brand designers, copywriters
- **Checklists and quick guides** — high save rate, good for lead magnets
- **Case studies (visual)** — results your clients got, formatted as a graphic

The underlying principle: show the outcome or the path to it.

---

## SEO and keyword tactics

Pinterest SEO is the main lever. Treat every pin like a search listing.

**Where to put keywords:**
- Pin title (under 100 characters, front-load the keyword)
- Pin description (natural use, most important keywords in the first 50-60 chars)
- Board names (be specific — "Content Strategy for Coaches" not "Marketing")
- Board descriptions
- Your profile bio

**How to find keywords:**
- Type your topic into Pinterest search and look at the autocomplete suggestions and the colored keyword bubbles that appear
- Use Pinterest Trends to catch seasonal searches
- Look at what boards top pins are saved to

**Cluster strategy:**
- Create 3-5 related pins around one theme and save them to the same keyword-rich board
- Publishing in clusters helps Pinterest connect your content and boosts the whole series

---

## Posting and consistency

- Aim for at least 2 pins per week to start; more is fine if quality holds
- Tuesday–Thursday around 10am tends to outperform weekends
- Consistency over 6-12 months is when compound traffic effects kick in
- Repurpose old pins by creating fresh graphics for the same content (Pinterest rewards new pin images even for existing URLs)

---

## Lead generation specifics

- **Seasonal lead magnets** — align freebies to what people are searching in a given season; can spike subscriber numbers fast
- **Pinterest-specific welcome emails** — acknowledge how they found you, follow up with relevant content for that entry point
- **Track outbound clicks in Pinterest Analytics** — find which pins actually drive traffic, double down on those formats and topics
- Most service providers see first email subscribers within 2-4 weeks of consistent activity

---

## Board setup

- Create boards around the problems your clients have, not the services you offer
  - e.g. "How to get more clients" not "My coaching services"
- Assign a category to every board so Pinterest can place it correctly
- Keep boards focused — a board with 20 highly relevant pins outperforms one with 200 random ones

---

## What doesn't work

- Pinning directly to a sales page (too cold, no context)
- Inconsistent posting then bursts (algorithm deprioritizes)
- Generic board names that don't match how people search
- Only pinning your own content (mix in repins of relevant others to signal your niche to the algorithm)
- Ignoring analytics — Pinterest tells you what's working, use it
