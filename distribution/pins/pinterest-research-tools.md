# Pinterest Research Tools + Niche Exploration

## The honest answer on Pinterest vs. Ahrefs/SEMrush

There is no direct Pinterest equivalent to Ahrefs or SEMrush. Pinterest does not expose raw search volume data the way Google does, so third-party tools work around this using signals like ad audience sizes, trends data, and engagement metrics. The tooling is less mature, but enough exists to do real research.

---

## Tools for Pinterest keyword and competitor research

### Native Pinterest (free)
- **Search bar autocomplete** — type a keyword and look at the dropdown suggestions; these are real searches people are making
- **Keyword bubbles** — after searching, colored pill buttons appear at the top with related subtopics; these reveal how Pinterest categorizes the niche
- **Pinterest Trends** (trends.pinterest.com) — shows relative search interest over time, seasonality, and rising topics. Free. Works similarly to Google Trends but Pinterest-specific.
- **Pinterest Ads Keyword Planner** — if you set up a business account and start creating an ad (you don't have to run it), you get access to a keyword planner that shows audience size estimates per keyword. This is the closest Pinterest gets to exposing search volume.

### Tailwind (freemium, Pinterest-native)
The most purpose-built tool for Pinterest keyword research.
- Combines six signals: Related Interests, Visual Search, Typeahead Suggestions, Pinterest Trends, Ads Audience Sizes, and Shopping Intent into a single "Resonance Score"
- Shows search volume where available
- Saved keyword library you can build over time
- Integrates directly with pin scheduling and creation
- Has a forever-free tier
- **Good for**: finding which keywords to target and building a keyword list for a niche

### Keywords Everywhere (browser extension, paid)
- Works on Pinterest search result pages
- Shows top pinners, average reactions, dominant colors of ranking pins, and verified pinners for a given search term
- Useful for understanding what a search results page actually looks like before you start optimizing for it

### PinClicks (paid)
- Keyword research with search volume and related keywords
- "Top Pins" view shows best-performing pins for any keyword
- Account Explorer for competitor tracking (their top boards and pins)
- **Good for**: understanding what content format and visual style is winning for a given search term

### PinRadar (paid)
- The main tool for competitor pin analytics — can analyze any pin on Pinterest, not just your own
- Real-time data, unlike Pinterest's own analytics (which has 24-48hr delay)
- **Good for**: spying on competitor performance at the individual pin level

### Pin Inspector (paid, desktop app)
- Scrapes Pinterest data for keyword and competitor research
- More technical but gives raw data on pins, saves, and engagement

### Ranktracker – Pinterest Search Volume Tool
- Standalone tool for checking Pinterest keyword search volume
- Useful for a quick lookup without committing to a full platform

---

## What you can't get (the gap vs. Ahrefs/SEMrush)

- No exact monthly search volume with confidence (Pinterest doesn't publish this)
- No backlink-style authority metrics for Pinterest accounts
- No historical ranking data for specific pins over time
- Competitor traffic estimates don't exist the way they do for websites

The workaround is to use Tailwind + Pinterest Trends + the Ads Planner together to triangulate relative demand.

## Exploring the niche: interactive apps for weddings

### Why this niche works on Pinterest

Wedding planning is one of Pinterest's top use cases. Brides and couples are active, high-intent searchers. They save obsessively and plan months or years ahead. The niche is visual, aspirational, and seasonal — all things Pinterest rewards. Technology/apps for weddings is a less saturated sub-niche within the giant wedding category.

### How to map the niche on Pinterest

**Step 1: Seed searches to run**
Start with these in the Pinterest search bar and note all autocomplete and bubble suggestions:
- "wedding app"
- "interactive wedding"
- "wedding games for guests"
- "wedding photo sharing"
- "wedding QR code"
- "digital wedding guestbook"
- "wedding seating chart app"
- "wedding website"
- "virtual wedding"
- "wedding guest experience"

**Step 2: Follow the bubbles**
After each search, write down every keyword bubble that appears. These reveal how Pinterest clusters this niche. Look for sub-niches you hadn't considered.

**Step 3: Check Pinterest Trends**
Go to trends.pinterest.com and check:
- Which of your seed keywords has consistent or growing interest
- Seasonality (wedding searches peak Jan–May as couples plan spring/summer weddings)
- "Rising" keywords — topics gaining traction you could get in early on

**Step 4: Find competitors**
Search your keywords and look at who ranks. Note:
- Which accounts appear repeatedly across multiple searches
- Their board structure (how they organize content)
- Which pins get the most saves
- Whether they're service providers, blogs, or product companies

Use PinClicks or PinRadar to go deeper on top-performing pins if you want data on saves and engagement.

**Step 5: Identify content gaps**
What are brides searching for that has thin or low-quality results? That's where you can own rankings fastest. The wedding tech sub-niche (apps, interactive experiences, QR codes, digital guestbooks) is less saturated than generic wedding content — there's likely room.

### What the audience is actually searching for (wedding tech angle)

Based on what's out there, the relevant audience searches for:
- Guest experience ideas ("how to keep wedding guests entertained")
- Photo sharing solutions ("wedding photo app guests")
- Interactive elements ("wedding games", "wedding activities for guests")
- Practical logistics ("wedding seating chart", "wedding day timeline app")
- Personalization ("custom wedding website", "wedding QR code ideas")

The buyer is the couple (or their planner), not the guests. Content should speak to couples solving problems, not guests using the experience.

### Board structure to consider for this niche

Organize boards around the problem, not your product:
- "Wedding guest experience ideas"
- "Interactive wedding ideas"
- "Tech-forward wedding planning"
- "Wedding photo sharing ideas"
- "Modern wedding planning tools"
- "Wedding games and activities"

Each board becomes a keyword-specific channel that can rank independently.

### Content angles that would work as pins

- "5 ways to get more guest photos at your wedding" → links to a blog post mentioning your app
- "What is a digital wedding guestbook?" → educational, evergreen, positions you as the expert
- "Before/after: paper guestbook vs. digital" → visual comparison
- "How [couple] got 300 guest photos from their wedding" → case study format
- Checklist: "Wedding day tech you didn't know you needed"
- Trend pieces: "The wedding trend taking over in 2026" (interactive photo experiences, etc.)
