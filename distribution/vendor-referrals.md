# Wedding-Vendor Referral Network

For a bespoke wedding reception service, this is likely the #1 distribution channel — bigger than SEO and GEO combined in year one. Bespoke wedding services propagate through trust, and trust in weddings runs through the couple's existing vendors.

Pair with `seo/strategy.md` and `geo/strategy.md`; those channels create the pull, this channel closes the loop.

---

## Why this dominates for bespoke services

- Couples ask their planner, photographer, videographer, DJ, and venue coordinator "who do you recommend for X?" — those recommendations convert at 40–60% (industry rule of thumb) vs 1–3% for cold search traffic.
- Vendors want to look modern and connected. If you make them look good at one wedding, they'll recommend you at the next ten.
- Weddings are geographic clusters — 20 planners in Berlin see each other at 50 weddings a year. Reputation spreads fast in both directions.
- Photo/video pros will feature your app in their reels and albums (free distribution) if it gave them a great scene to shoot.

---

## Who to build relationships with (priority order)

1. **Wedding planners / coordinators** — the single biggest lever. They're picking every vendor for the couple and want to look creative. One planner relationship = 5–20 weddings/year potential.
2. **Photographers and videographers** — control the visual narrative. If your app produces beautiful moments they get to shoot, they'll actively suggest it to future couples so they get more moments like it. Also: their portfolios feature your app, feeding your own social + GEO.
3. **Venue coordinators** — especially at modern/design-forward venues. Same logic as planners; they see every couple who books that venue.
4. **DJs and MCs** — natural partners because your game runs during their set. An MC who can pitch a live trivia moment gets more bookings. Offer a rev share or spiff.
5. **Wedding stationers / branding designers** — smaller volume but overlap heavily with your buyer persona (design-conscious couples).
6. **Bands** — lower priority; more culturally distinct from tech.

Skip: florists, cake makers, dress shops. Overlap is thin.

## Directory listings (do these first — they're passive lead sources)

**DACH:**
- **Weddyplace** — premium listing worthwhile; strong DE SEO. https://weddyplace.com
- **Hochzeitsplaza** — solid DACH directory. https://www.hochzeitsplaza.de
- **Weddix** — mid-tier, cheap listing. https://www.weddix.de
- **Marry** (Austria/Germany magazine) — print + web. Getting a Real Wedding feature is worth more than the listing.
- **Frieda Theres** — high-end DE editorial. Feature-based, not directory, but critical for taste-maker audience.

**International (list if you take destination weddings):**
- **The Knot Vendor** — huge US pipeline, worth it if you serve destination weddings. https://www.theknot.com
- **Zola Vendor** — similar. https://www.zola.com/wedding-vendors
- **WeddingWire** — same parent as The Knot, dual-list. https://www.weddingwire.com
- **Junebug Weddings** — editorial, not directory; feature-based. https://junebugweddings.com
- **Rock My Wedding** (UK) — editorial. https://www.rockmywedding.co.uk
- **Green Wedding Shoes** — editorial, US-based, modern taste. https://greenweddingshoes.com

Prioritize 2 DACH listings + 2 editorial-feature pitches in the first month. Skip low-tier directories that require heavy annual fees for negligible traffic — check listed vendors' review counts and last-activity dates before paying.

## The vendor-relationship playbook

### 1. Warm intro over cold pitch, always
Get introduced through past-wedding couples, mutual venue contacts, or your existing photographer/planner connections. Cold outreach to wedding vendors has a ~5% response rate; warm intros ~60%.

### 2. Free first wedding for a top target
Pick 3 aspirational planners or venues in your city. Offer to build a custom app for one of their weddings **at cost** (or free) in exchange for:
- Being featured in their vendor recommendations for 12 months
- Photos/video from that wedding for your portfolio
- An intro to two other planners in their network

Frame it as market research, not desperation.

### 3. Kickback structure (be explicit)
- **10–15% referral fee** to planners for booked weddings is standard in adjacent categories (photo booth, live entertainment).
- Some planners prefer a flat spiff (€150–€300) per booked lead; others want a percentage. Ask directly.
- Some prefer no money and just want to look good — respect that and send them a nice bottle after each successful wedding instead.
- Track it in a simple spreadsheet: vendor, lead date, booked date, amount, paid date. Pay within 7 days of the wedding — reliability is the moat.

### 4. Make it easy for them to sell you
Send each vendor partner:
- A one-page PDF with 3 sample games, price band, and 3 quotes from past couples
- 5–10 short vertical videos they can send in DMs when a couple asks "what's cool for the reception?"
- A single booking link with their name pre-filled so attribution is automatic

Most vendors won't remember to sell you unless the pitch is ready-made. Do the work for them.

### 5. Show up
- Attend 1–2 local wedding industry events per quarter (bridal fairs, planner network meetups, venue open houses).
- Follow every planner/photographer you meet on Instagram within 24h; comment on their next 3 posts genuinely.
- Send a hand-written thank-you card after every wedding you work — to the planner, not just the couple. This is old-fashioned and completely unmatched.

### 6. Real Weddings features are compound interest
When you build for a wedding with great photography, submit the story to:
- **Junebug Weddings** — https://junebugweddings.com/submit-your-wedding
- **Green Wedding Shoes** — submissions via their site
- **Style Me Pretty** — submissions via their site
- **Frieda Theres** (DE)
- **Rock My Wedding** (UK)

Feature pitches need a full gallery + wedding narrative + your product's role. Coordinate with the photographer — they usually want the feature too and will lead the submission if you ask.

A single Junebug feature = permanent backlink + LLM training data + Pinterest fuel + planner-audience credibility. Highest-leverage single action on this page.

## Vendor-network CRM (minimum viable)

Airtable or Notion, three tables:

1. **Vendors** — name, role (planner/photog/DJ/venue/MC), city, first met, last touch, warmth (cold/warm/hot), notes
2. **Leads** — couple name, wedding date, source vendor, discovery call date, quote sent, status, value
3. **Payouts** — vendor, lead ID, wedding date, amount, paid date

Review weekly. If a vendor has sent 3+ leads that closed, they're a "champion" — protect that relationship above all else. Send a Christmas gift.

## What NOT to do

- Don't cold-email 200 wedding planners at once. Reputation in this industry is small and negative signals spread faster than positive ones.
- Don't undercut or badmouth other vendors — you'll be uninvited from the recommendation lists that matter.
- Don't ghost after one wedding — this is a repeat-referral business. Every wedding is the start of the next 5.
- Don't pay for listings without checking recent-activity signals. Some DE directories have gone stagnant; the fee isn't the issue, the wasted month is.
- Don't chase national planners in year one. Local density beats national spread — 5 planners in your city outreferring you > 30 planners nationwide who forget you exist.

## 90-day plan

**Month 1**
- 2 DACH directory listings live (Weddyplace + one other).
- 3 warm-intro coffee meetings with local planners.
- Identify 3 aspirational venues in your city; get on their vendor recommendation lists.
- One-page PDF + 5 short vertical videos ready to send.

**Month 2**
- Free/at-cost wedding for 1 aspirational planner, IF the calendar allows.
- 2 Real Wedding submissions to editorial sites (with photographer coordination).
- Attend 1 local industry event.
- CRM live with all contacts from month 1.

**Month 3**
- Formal referral-fee structure documented and shared with active vendor partners.
- First payouts made within 7 days of the weddings.
- Review: which of the month-1 planners have actually sent a lead? Double down on the responsive ones, deprioritize the rest.
- 2 more editorial submissions.

---

## The one metric that matters

**Number of active referring vendors in the last 90 days.**

Not leads, not bookings — vendors who've sent you a lead recently. 5 active referrers ≈ enough for a full year of bookings in year one. 15 active referrers = a sustainable business.

If that number is flat, more marketing spend won't fix it — go have coffee with more planners.
